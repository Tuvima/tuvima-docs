# Current Presentation and Product Rules

These rules describe current, product-owner-approved behaviour. They were moved out of the always-loaded `CLAUDE.md` so that sessions read them only when working on the affected surfaces. Engineering ownership rules for the Dashboard live in `src/MediaEngine.Web/CLAUDE.md`. When rules conflict, the most recent dated section wins.

## Grouping, cards, Home, and detail pages

Current product rule: a Series is the lane-level shelf shown in Read, Watch, or Listen. A broader Universe/Collection is shown on `/collections` only when a shared series/franchise/universe relationship connects multiple shelves. Watch has separate `TV Shows` and `Series` shelves: TV show identities never share the movie Series row, while Series contains only movie groups dynamically aligned from trusted library metadata. TV shows may appear as show cards with one or more owned episodes, but non-TV/non-music series cards require at least two distinct owned works by default (`lane_group_display.*.minimum_series_items`). TV Timeline groups a show by its canonical premiere year, never the newest owned episode year; Network grouping renders the resolved network logo in the same identity position used for person-led group cards. A single owned movie/book/audiobook remains a normal item tile in its respective lane; audiobooks belong in Listen, not Read. A lane shelf such as owning multiple Matrix films stays in Watch without duplicating itself as a top-level Collection. Multiple formats of one work are variants, not collection triggers. Internal collection IDs may back a lane group, but user-facing surfaces must name and route by the group type: a TV show is a show, a book sequence is a series, and a curated rollup is a collection/list. Book, comic, and movie series cards preserve structural order while selecting at most four representative preview items; broader collections prefer cross-media breadth. Non-TV series and collection containers render through the fixed-size landscape `MediaGroupTile`: rest is artwork-led, with two to four slightly angled, overlapping images using actual portrait, square, or wide metadata. Home and Discover hover preserve the exact artwork and dimensions, add the purple boundary/glow, and reveal only a lightly shaded compact top overlay for type, title, and one status line. Direct grids instead embed an all-caps title, year, and owned count inside the bottom of the card; hover adds the glow and a small open cue without a type pill or cinematic replacement. There is no permanent copy column, child-title list, carousel, or child-level navigation. The retired artwork carousel and rotating collage must not be restored. The entire group card opens the group. TV shows remain on `MediaTile`; cinematic backdrop, logo, facts, and description hover is limited to Home and Discover. Existing item tiles remain on `MediaTile`.

**Videos (Watch).** Videos is the plain Watch kind (nothing is looked up) for courses, talks and downloads; it can hold several named libraries. Personal and phone footage belongs in View's Personal videos, not here. Its tab on Watch shows standalone videos and series (a folder of videos, with optional sections for sub-folders) as cards. A series opens the show page and a standalone video opens the movie page, so Resume, Up Next, progress and Mark watched work as they do for TV. A Videos folder is read from its layout and local files only (YouTube ID in the name or `.info.json`, `.nfo`, then the cleaned file name); the YouTube lookup (YouTube kind only) sends only the video ID. Saved videos have no ratings, so a Kid's profile sees them only while the household's **Show unrated items** switch is on, like every other unrated item. An item or series marked Explicit is never shown to a Kid's profile.

**Browse tabs and Unmatched (Watch, Listen, Read).** Each lane shows one tab for every kind that has folders (Movies, TV Shows, YouTube, Music, Audiobooks, Books, Comics) and one for every named custom library (for example Lectures), in that order; a kind or library with no folders has no tab, and a person only sees the tabs for libraries they may see. Each library tab has an Unmatched filter chip that anyone who can see the library may use; it lists that library's files the Engine could not match, flat, with no grouping. Administrators get Retry now, Keep as is and Look up again in an Unmatched item's "..." menu on its page; Keep as is asks first and the item stays under Unmatched, marked Kept (and out of the Unmatched count), until it is looked up again. Unmatched files never appear in the lanes, on Home, in shelves or in collections, and Search lists them in a separate "Unmatched" group, found by file name and tags, never mixed into the matched results.

Every card is one semantic link to its detail surface and contains no inline Read, Play, Listen, My List, reaction, remove, or details controls. Vertically wrapping `MediaTileGrid` results preserve the exact resting cover or group artwork on hover and keyboard focus, show a compact title and optional year below the art, and add only a stronger purple glow—no hover identity strip, scrim, background replacement, or popover. Direct browse cards omit redundant media/group pills such as `TV Show` inside the TV Shows route because the active navigation already supplies that context. Their filter areas provide a tile-size slider that changes width while retaining portrait, square, and landscape ratios; Music defaults smaller than the other formats. Movie and TV cinematic landscape expansion is reserved for Home and lane Discover shelves when background artwork supports it; its left-aligned rating, classification, year, and runtime row uses a compact translucent backing that hugs the text, with no inline actions. Touch never shows a hover preview or hover strip: a tap opens details, and a tile whose title only the preview carried (cinematic Movie/TV cards) shows its compact title and year under the art instead. On phones shelf rows run edge to edge with a 16 px gutter and cards are sized from the screen width, about 2.6 portrait or 2.4 square cards across and landscape series, collection, group and Continue Watching cards at 86% of the screen (at most 340 px, 320 px for Continue Watching), so the next card always peeks and no card is wider than the screen.

**Phone Home shelves.** Continue Across Media is one horizontal row on phones (tablets and desktops keep their per-type groups): newest activity first, started items only (an item never started is not in it), each card with its art, title, a thin progress bar only when there is real progress (never at zero), a small type icon and one line saying where you are (S1 E2 · 23 min left, Chapter 4, 42% read). A tap opens the card's normal destination. When nothing has been started the whole section, header included, is not shown. Every Home shelf header (Recently Added, Watch, Read, Listen) reads title on the left and See All on the right as a 48 px target. The Recently Added filter chips (All, Watch, Read, Listen, View) stay in one row and scroll sideways instead of wrapping. Items still being matched keep their Still being matched tag, are drawn smaller and quieter, and sort after the matched items.

Home alone uses `CinematicHeroCarousel`/`CinematicHeroSurface` as a landing treatment; detail heroes continue sharing `DetailHeroContent`, and both use `HeroBackdrop` for foreground cover/album geometry and book decoration. Home uses an approximately 80svh desktop feature (72svh on short screens), with bounded cover-led composition for unknown, portrait or undersized sources; measured landscapes use the aspect-band framing policy. Phones and short landscape screens use content-sized composition. Detail heroes retain `95svh`. Opposite edge arrows have 48px targets; the centered active dash is thicker, wider, and purple. Focus, hover, pause, and reduced motion suspend rotation. On touch the hero also moves with a horizontal swipe of more than 48 px (less vertical travel), and rotation is held while a finger is down. Home has no lane submenu. Its rows are Continue Across Media, Recently Added, then populated Watch, Read, Listen, and Collections & Lists; Fresh is retired. Recent filters All/Watch/Read/Listen/View retain the selected scope in `/recently-added?type=` and use added-at keyset paging. View contributes only independently authorized Mine assets, with fresh render-time media grants and native viewer navigation. Untouched TV features use show art; started Home TV keeps the show title, shows `Sx Ey · Episode Title`, and uses the exact owned episode still/synopsis/action with show-art fallback. Watch discovery remains rooted in show art. Shared explicit subject/state/episode contracts drive Engine spotlight selection, Continue, and sequence detail. Only partial long-form media has a purple artwork-edge progress strip; albums, tracks, and View omit completion bars. Resting movies/TV/books/comics use portrait frames, albums and audiobooks use square frames, and only Continue Watching uses landscape stills. Artwork is contained when its native ratio differs from its frame. Home shelves share one artwork height (on phones about 2.6 portrait cards across); only Home Watch discovery cards expand horizontally in their row; Watch browse grids, episode lists, and non-Watch cards remain outline-only. Cards retain one semantic detail link and partial long-form artwork-edge progress, without detached percentage captions. Read, Watch, Listen, and Collections retain their compact shared route navigation, viewport-anchored desktop rails, unboxed scoped filters, and existing downstream browsing behavior. On phones the filters on Watch, Read, Listen, Collections and Search collapse into one *Filters (n)* button that opens a bottom sheet holding the same controls (with Clear and a sticky *Show N items* button; filters still apply live), while the browse-mode strip scrolls sideways with a fade edge and the card-size slider is labelled in words (*Card size: Medium*); tablets and desktops keep the inline filter panel. With no hero, Home content starts below the top bar. A tile for an item still being matched or enriched shows a small soft dot before its caption title, with the tooltip "Still being matched"; the dot does not pulse under reduced motion. Where a tile shows a hover card, Movie/TV cards use the landscape banner layout only when real landscape art exists; otherwise the portrait art layout.

**Phone Home hero (under 600 px).** Phones replace the full-bleed carousel with `PhoneHeroCard`: one rounded, inset card whose height is the same for every slide and media type (about 55% of the space between the top bar and the bottom bar), so the first shelf header never moves and always sits above the bottom bar. The card has fixed slots: picture, title (two lines at most), one meta line, one full-width primary button (48 px at least) and at most two 48 px icon buttons (My List, More; Rate and the thumbs live in More). The picture ladder is a landscape backdrop with the title logo over it when there is one, then the cover at its own shape (it only ever shrinks), then a tinted placeholder tile that names the media type. The Engine's stand-in cover is a 100 by 150 picture with no flag of its own, so a cover whose measured width is unknown or under 240 px counts as no artwork and gets the tile, never a stand-in drawn at its natural size; phones ask for the sized (`m`) rendition. Nothing rotates by itself: no timer, no arrows, no pause button; swipe and the labelled dots change slides, and the dots are real buttons inside a region with an accessible name. An item still being matched gets the placeholder (or its picture) plus a small "Still being matched" tag. The meta line comes from `PhoneHeroPresentation.MetaLine` (never raw decimal minutes: 101 min reads 1h 41m; right plurals; empty or zero parts hidden; movie year, runtime, rating; TV year and seasons, an episode S2 E6 and year; book author, year, pages; album artist, tracks, time; audiobook author, length). The title sits in a fixed two-line logo zone: a logo (however wide or tall) scales to fit inside it and a title with no logo is drawn as text in it, so the card never changes height. The primary button reads by type from the data the Engine already sends: unstarted Play (movie), Watch S1 E1 (TV, from the episode or the Engine's label), Start Reading (book, comic), Listen (audiobook), Play album (music); started Resume S5 E1 with the time left (movie, episode), Continue Reading with the percent read, Continue Listening with the time left. Continue, Resume and "0%" never appear on an unstarted item, a time or percent suffix is shown only when the Engine supplied the number, and the button stays one 48 px line with an ellipsis. An album always plays from its first track because the hero carries no track position. The hero shows no synopsis: the text zone is full and a synopsis would either cover the picture or change the card height. Tablets and desktops keep the carousel above.

Canonical media, person, series, and standard-collection details use a full-width `95svh` desktop cinematic stage so its lower navigation remains visible while native 16:9 artwork loses as little of the frame as possible. Edge-to-edge landscape art is pinned top-center beneath the translucent global app bar and translucent lower navigation; the bottom is deliberately darkened more heavily than the top so the source's upper composition and hero copy remain legible. Music albums use the shared hero and lower navigation: the cover supplies a blurred atmosphere, a large sharp album/record composition is centered in the right half, identity and actions grow upward from the shared lower-left anchor, a compact Play button and circular Shuffle/My List/Rate/More controls share the action row, and MusicAlbum exposes **Overview** plus **Details**. Overview uses a proportional Tracks/Credits layout, keeps the borderless list with Show missing and no search, resolves identified artists into the same compact square clickable people cards used by movie and TV credits, and conditionally shows every other owned album from the exact canonical primary artist in a full-width **More by** shelf. Oversized provider box-set manifests are scoped to the canonical disc that represents the tagged local album; ordinary multi-disc albums retain their full sequence. Technical and source content belongs only to Details. Audiobooks retain the shared detail structure, chapter-level playback, and specialized player tools, use a substantially larger cover where viewport height permits, and keep Restart in More alongside the other shared actions. Audiobook Chapters remain embedded; Editions does not return, and lists contain neither Play All nor a second My List action. Structural details place their ordered sequence, collection, or owned-works array below the stage on Overview only, ending with an owned summary and an authoritative-total progress bar when available. Collections use a neutral no-backdrop hero and the same up-to-four representative artwork cluster as collection tiles at a dominant scale; they suppress contributor credits, expose only a functional Shuffle action, and use a compact row for the year range, total items, and applicable Read/Watch/Listen counts. Books and comics share a larger cover-only foreground envelope, as do poster-only movie/TV heroes; true landscape backdrops remain edge-to-edge. People use the same title, role-credit, facts, and synopsis typography as other detail pages, keep only the portrait treatment person-specific, and render one continuous page without tabs. Linked identities reuse cast/credit portrait cards. Explicit collective pseudonyms are the displayed and attributed author identity; linked real identities are not promoted unless explicitly credited on another work. Real multiple-author bylines retain order and use semicolons followed by one space. Person music credits collapse tracks into one album card and show roles/year without track counts. Role and lane filters render only when more than one real option exists. Utility actions use larger icon-left-of-label controls on a left-growing row beneath the primary action. Jump to appears beside the array heading only above ten entries; beside a Series heading on wider screens it is as wide as its longest entry, so entries do not wrap; a movie Series is one full-width rail of every movie (other runs page six at a time); every full-width row below the hero (the Series, episode or owned-works row, Also in this world, More by) is headed by the one shared collapse arrow (`AppCollapsibleRow`): collapsed it is a slim bar with its title and count, the person's open or closed choice is remembered per kind of row in that browser, and a movie's Series row starts collapsed only when it has nothing else to show (no other owned movie, no missing movie for Show missing, no other Series to switch to); track lists keep their scrollbar space, so Show missing never shifts the columns; alternate series/arc selectors align left and Show missing aligns right. Person works use managed portrait covers/posters or square album art, never cinematic landscape backgrounds, and prefer owned-asset canonical titles over parent collection labels. Overview combines attributed description or biography, a purpose-built cast/credits peek, and applicable series or collection context; Detail tabs per type: movie, TV show and season **Overview, Cast, Details**; episode **Overview, Cast, Details**; album **Overview, Details**; audiobook **Overview, Tracks, Details**; book and comic **Overview, Credits, Details**; series types and Collection **Overview, Details**; a Universe page **Overview** (with the owned items in it) and **Details**; a person one page with no tabs. Cast shows each actor with the role name they played, and links to the person. There is no Character page, no Characters, Portrayals or Relationships tab and no View Appearances button; character and place links across formats belong to the Universe plan. No detail page has a Universe or Related tab, on any device; the Universe explorer and Collections stay reachable from their own places, and a stale `?tab=universe` or `?tab=related` link falls back to the first tab. **Details** reads top to bottom: file facts, then History. File facts are whatever the data store already holds per item (file name and format, container, length, pages, video, audio and subtitle summary, language, and per file the size such as `1.4 GB` or `312 MB`, the date it was added such as `2026-10-16`, the bitrate such as `6.2 Mbps` or `256 kbps`, and the audio and subtitle tracks when more than the summary already says); everything comes from stored rows and nothing is probed on page open, so a fact that is not stored is simply left out, shown on every device as a compact two-column list that wraps on phones, with one block per file when an item has several; groupings (shows, seasons, albums, series, collections) own no file and point to the items inside. When an AI feature plugin (Vibe tags, Description insights) has left anything for the item, a **Suggested by Local AI** panel marked AI follows the Details list with its short summary, vibes, themes, moods and content warnings; with the plugins off nothing AI shows anywhere. Library browse pages add **Vibes** and **Themes** filters beside Genre, listed only when at least one item has any, and carried in the page address as `vibe` and `theme`. The file path and History appear only to people who are already offered the Edit action, and History is a read-only list shown only on desktop-class screens, read from `GET /library/items/{id}/history`. Standard collection details use the same resolved membership as the Collections catalog. Listen playlists remain specialized lane-local surfaces. Every list of songs is the same table: All songs, Albums, an album page's tracks and a playlist. Its header and rows look and behave alike everywhere: tick box, number (an equalizer mark on the playing song, whose title turns the accent color), cover, title, Artist, Album, Date added, Plays, Genre, a heart, time, and a **Columns** button as the last header cell. Which columns exist depends on the place (Songs has all but Genre; an album page has number, title, time, status, the heart and an Artist column only when the album has more than one artist; a playlist has number, title, Artist, Album, the heart and time). **Columns** lists the ones the place offers; you can hide, show and resize them, and **Reset columns** puts the defaults back. Your column and width choices are kept on your profile for each place, so they follow you to another device. A single click on a row plays it. The heart saves the song to Favorites (never My List); a missing album track has no heart or actions. A click on a header sorts (Date added, Plays, Time and the heart start with the largest first); an album page keeps numbered tracks first and its Unnumbered group last whatever is sorted, and a playlist is in your own order, so its headers do not sort. Add to queue appears on hover or keyboard focus, before the row's "..." menu (Play next, Add to queue, Go to album, Go to artist, listened status, then Edit and Delete from library for administrators on a desktop; Remove from playlist in a playlist). In a playlist you can edit, drag a row by the handle that replaces its number (a purple line shows where it will land), or move the focused row with Alt+Up and Alt+Down (the move is announced); on touch, **Move up** and **Move down** are in the row's "..." menu. On phones every song list is a plain list with no card boxes: cover, title with the artist beneath, and "...", in 64px rows (an album page's list has no covers and shows the track number, with the artist under a title only when it differs from the album artist). Tapping a row plays it; the "..." bottom sheet has Add to Favorites (the heart is not on the row) and the same actions as the desktop menu, with Move up, Move down (disabled at the ends) and a destructive Remove in a playlist. The phone Songs page has a header with a search field, Play, Shuffle, and one **Sort and filter** button that opens a bottom sheet (Sort by, and the filters the page already has); your sort is kept on your profile, and an A to Z strip with letter headings appears while sorted by Title or Artist. Long song lists load as you scroll. Nothing on touch depends on drag or hover, and drag handles and column resize grips appear only with a mouse at the desktop width. Below 1024px the now-playing bar's title block and transport share the width instead of holding fixed widths. On phones (the phone form factor) the hero is sized to its content under the top bar instead of filling the screen: the primary action is full width and 48px high, My List, Rate and More are 44px round icons with their names underneath, fact rows wrap as chips, genre chips keep a 44px tap box, and More opens a bottom sheet listing the same actions. The section tabs keep a 112px minimum width, fade at the edge that still has tabs and scroll the active tab into view; the episode and works rails swipe with the next card peeking, credits and people stay in one or two columns, and track rows fit the screen without sideways scrolling. Tablet and desktop keep the composition above.

A detail page never shows a button that goes nowhere: the main button exists only when there is something to play or read (Watch, Resume, Read, Listen, Play album, Explore Timeline), so a page with nothing playable, such as a track with no readable or listenable format, a show with no owned episode or a universe without a timeline, has no main button and no generic "Open" fallback. Every action the Engine sends either carries a route to open or is one the Dashboard handles itself (`DetailActionKeys`), and a test fails when one does neither. A detail page that cannot open says why in one of three ways: "not found" (also used for items outside the active profile's access), "not allowed", or "Engine unreachable" with a Retry button; only the connection failure offers Retry.

## Extras (October 2026)

Extras are the guidebooks, homework, picture folders and clips that sit beside a show, film, album, book, comic or course. In the editor they are called **supporting files**: each is an editable item with a title, description, group, role, "Belongs to" (the show, a season or an episode) and an optional "Shown alongside" episode or lesson. They appear as one row called Extras on the title's page, under its seasons or cast, and the row is hidden when there are none (there is no empty state and no setting).

- **What counts.** One rule everywhere. A file is an Extra when it sits in a title's folder but is not one of the library's own files for that kind (an episode, film, track, book or issue), or when its name or place says so (a trailer, a featurette, a "Behind the scenes" or "Extras" folder). A numbered file in a show or course folder stays an episode, and a PDF stays a comic issue or a book unless its name or place says it is an extra. Cover art, posters and subtitles are not Extras.
- **Cards.** A guidebook card says "PDF, 42 pages", a picture folder is one card ("24 pictures") however many pictures it holds, and a clip card shows its length. A file that has gone missing stays as a card with a warning and "File not found". On a phone the row scrolls sideways; on a TV each card takes focus.
- **Opening.** A guidebook opens in the browser's own reader (PDF and plain text; an EPUB guidebook downloads), a picture folder opens as a gallery with a full-size view, and a clip plays in a plain player. Going back returns to the title's page. An Extra clip is never an episode: playing it never changes watched marks, progress or next-up.
- **Who sees them.** Extras follow their title: a profile that cannot see the title never sees its Extras, Kids levels apply to the title as a whole, and a title in the Vault has no Extras. Deleting a title leaves the files alone, and a show's Extras go from the page with it. Backups keep a person's decisions about Extras, not the ones the rule makes again on its own.
- **Naming and placement.** Library reads where a file belongs, what it is and what to call it from its folder and file name (a file in a season folder belongs to that season; one named after an episode belongs to that episode; in a course, to the course, a section or the lesson whose number or words it shares), with the words kept in `config/extras.json`. [Naming supporting files](../guides/naming-supporting-files.md) lists them. Archives, `.nfo` files, subtitles and other sidecars are never supporting files.
- **Editing (desktop editor only).** Open a supporting file from the item dropdown or the browser. Details holds Title, Description (**Suggest details from the document** fills both on the person's machine, with no provider), a type-in **Group** box that offers **Create "Week 1"** for a new name, **Role** (a short list that suits the file's type), **Belongs to** (with a hint saying "From its folder location" or "Set by you" and a **Use folder location** link) and **Shown alongside**, and Tags. Changing **Belongs to** moves the file in a Managed folder (with the amber footer warning first) and only changes its place in the Library in a Read-only folder; nothing else about a supporting file moves or rewrites it. Up to eight supporting files at a place show in the item dropdown; above that **All supporting files (N)** opens the browser (search, a Where filter, a type filter, groups with counts, a table of 25 rows a page, and bulk Set group, Set role, Belongs to, Add tags and Delete on ticked rows; **Edit selected** opens "Editing N files"). On the details page the Extras row shows at most 12 cards per group, with **See all N**; a file shown alongside an episode or lesson also appears as a chip under it ("Worksheet · PDF"), and on a course the player's end card adds **Homework for this lesson** with an Open button. Main search finds supporting files under their own heading. The numbers (8, 12, 25) are settings in `config/editor.json`.
- **Fixing a mistake (desktop editor only).** In the browser's **Folders and files** view, **What is this file?** tells Library what a file is: an episode (or track, or book), a supporting file with a role and a place, something that belongs to another title, or a file to ignore. The role is guessed from the file name and only fills in the choice. Every choice stages a change, Review changes shows it ("The file stays where it is"), and Save changes does it. Making a clip an episode adds the file as that episode and the episode count goes up; making an episode a supporting file removes it from the Library (its progress is lost) and keeps the file. Ignored files are hidden from the Library and future scans, listed in Settings > Libraries > Excluded files as "Ignored by you" with **Add back**, and never deleted. A change is never undone by Discard after saving; sort the file again the same way.

## Picture sizes (right-sized pictures, October 2026)

Every picture loads at about the size it is drawn, never the downloaded original, except the artwork picker's full view and downloads. The rule: ask for the smallest copy (tiny 96, small 320, medium 960, large 2160 px on the long side) that is at least the drawn size times the screen's sharpness. Fixed-size pictures offer the next copy up to sharp screens; screen-wide pictures let the browser choose medium on phones and large on desktop and TV. A picture seen once is kept by the browser and not fetched again until it changes; an edited picture gets a new address and shows straight away. Photos in View keep their own sizes (160-2048).

| Surface | Drawn (CSS px, long side) | Copy asked for |
|---|---|---|
| People list, Universe cast lists, family tree, Events/Added rows, table and song-row covers, Continue strip | 32-64 | tiny (96); small on sharp screens |
| Now playing bar, queue and history rows, editor thumbnails | 48-128 | tiny or small by drawn size; next copy up on sharp screens |
| Credit card faces and character pictures | up to 140 | small |
| Home, Watch, Listen, Read tiles and shelves | 128-342 | small; medium on sharp screens (browser choice) |
| Search results, Listen album/artist art, Series and album rails | 160-400 | small or medium by drawn size |
| Tile hover panel, details cover, person portrait, full player, editor artwork preview | 320-640 | medium |
| Hero carousel, details and phone hero backgrounds | full width | medium on phones, large on desktop and TV (browser choice) |
| Artwork picker "full view" and downloads | full | original |

Logos have no copies and load as stored. A new screen that draws an Engine picture without a size fails the build (`PictureSizeGuardrailTests`).

## Shelves and View all grids (October 2026)

Approved by the product owner on 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-20.

**Shelves.** Every Home and lane shelf of titles shows up to 20 items (author and genre shelves too, still at most three of each). Every shelf with a See all link ends with a **View all** tile as tall as the row's tiles (arrow and the words View all, a 48 px minimum target, reachable with a keyboard or TV remote, named "View all {shelf}"); the header link stays (See All on phones, View all elsewhere). The tile and the link go to the same place: the shelf's View all grid, or, for shelves whose own page already lists everything, that page (Series, Artists, Playlists, Favorite Songs, audiobook series, Collections & Lists, and the Collections and For Me shelves).

**Which shelves have a grid.** Home Continue Across Media, Watch, Read and Listen; Read's Continue reading, Recently added and author shelves; Watch's Continue watching, TV Shows, Movies and genre shelves; Listen's Continue listening, Recently played, recently updated albums, recently added audiobooks, New tracks added and Albums (the music shelves all open the albums grid in their own order); and Liked. My List has its own page (`/my-list`, see *My List page*) rather than a View all grid. Each grid is at `/all/{lane}/{shelf}` and shows the shelf's whole list, not just the first 20.

**The grid.** The shelf's title as the heading and a count ("312 titles"), then sort and filters, then tiles that load more as the person scrolls (a **Load more** button stays under the grid for keyboards, remotes and screen readers). TV episodes appear as one show card and tracks as one album card, exactly as on the shelf.

**Sorts.** Recently added (the default), Title A–Z, Year (newest first, unknown years last) and Recently played (most recent first; never-played titles last, newest first). Continue and Recently played grids open on Recently played. On My List and Liked, Recently added means when the title was saved or liked. The sort chosen on a grid is remembered for the profile, on every device, per lane: Watch, Read, Listen, My List and Liked each keep their own. Continue and Recently played grids always open in their own order; re-sorting them lasts for that visit only. If the sort can't be saved it still applies for that visit and a small notice says so.

**Filters.** Only the filters that mean something for that list are shown, and a filter with fewer than two choices is hidden: **Media type** (on mixed lists such as Home's Watch, Read and Listen, My List and Liked; My List and Liked also offer Collections), **Genre** (not on a genre shelf's grid), **Format** (EPUB, PDF and comic files for reading; 4K, HD and SD for watching; never for Listen) and **Progress** (Not started, In progress, Finished; never on Continue or Recently played grids, and not while the list includes music, which has no progress). A title matches a format when any copy the person may see has it; a show's progress comes from its episodes. Each choice shows its count. On phones the sort and filters sit in the shared **Filters** bottom sheet ("Show N titles"); elsewhere they sit above the grid. Chosen filters show as removable chips with Clear all. Sort and filters are kept in the page address (replacing the history entry, so they add no Back steps), so reload and Back return to the same view.

**States.** The first load holds a skeleton grid the size of the tiles. A list that a filter empties says "Nothing matches these filters" with Clear filters; an empty list says "Nothing here yet". A grid that doesn't exist says "This list isn't available" with a link Home. A failed page keeps what is loaded and offers Try again.

**Who sees what.** Grids are read from the same authorized projection as lane pages: the profile's Kid's levels (for a Kid's profile), its lane (area) access and the libraries hidden from its household or from the profile apply, Documents never appear, a format from a copy in a library the account can't see is never offered or counted, and My List and Liked only ever show the signed-in profile's own lists (another profile's private collection or playlist never appears even if its id was saved). The TV app's "See all" reads the same full list.

## Home page: top banner, pick-up row, filters and hover (October 2026)

Approved by the product owner on 2026-10-27 (Home page redesign spec, decisions D1 to D6).

**Top banner (the hero).** It holds at most five slides. Everything the person is in the middle of comes first, most recently touched first, then newly added items, newest first. A show is one slide: a show with an episode started shows that episode (Resume, or Watch Next Episode when the last one is finished) with the show's logo; a show never started shows the series with "Start watching". Book covers in the banner are the same size as on the item's own page. Only items that have finished matching and have real artwork appear. The banner is built in two steps: the person's settings decide which items are allowed, then an orderer puts only those in order. The default orderer is the order above; a plugin's suggestion can lift a new item inside the "new" group but never ahead of something in progress. If a smarter orderer is added later and is off or fails, the default order applies.

**Banner settings (Settings, Preferences, Home).** Each profile can turn the banner off (Home then starts at its first row), choose one to five slides, allow or block Watch, Read and Listen items, and choose "continued plus new" or "continued only". They only narrow what the banner picks from. The Engine applies them, so phones, TVs and browsers agree.

**Plugin slides.** A plugin may suggest an item for the banner with a short reason. The slide is drawn by the Library, labelled "Suggested by {plugin}" (plus "AI" when Local AI produced it) with the reason on hover, and only appears for an item that already passes every check above. Text with markup, script or an address, and icons that are not a plain icon name, are refused and the reason is logged. Each profile can switch a plugin's slides off.

**Pick up where you left off.** One horizontal row of cards, one card per thing started, whatever its type (a show once, an album as "Recently played"). Each card has the cover, the type with its icon, the title, a subtitle, a progress bar with a line such as "42% watched", and a round play button. The title opens the item's page. With nothing started the row is not shown.

**Recently Added filters.** The pills are toggles: All, Watch, Read, Listen, View. A type with nothing in the library has no pill, and two or more can be on at once. The tiles are 25 percent smaller than shelf tiles.

**One hover (superseded in November 2026, see "Home, detail and tiles" below).** No tile grows, lifts or turns landscape on hover any more: the cover gets a thick white border with a purple ring, and keyboard focus looks the same. This keeps neighbours still, so the page does not jump while you move the mouse along a row.

**Opening an item.** Clicking a tile hands its cover, logo and backdrop to the item's page, so the page shows the real picture and title straight away instead of grey boxes while the rest loads. A link typed or reloaded keeps the grey stand-ins.

## When an item appears: Ready

Every library surface (Home, the hero, the shelves and lanes, Watch, Read, Listen, collections, People pages and the Library list) shows an item only when it is **Ready**. An item is Ready when it has been matched to a real title, is placed in its show or album (an episode under its show, a track under its album; books and comics that belong to no series stand alone), has its artwork (found, or searched for and settled), is not waiting in Needs Review, and has not been rejected. Wikidata and the later enrichment steps are not required, so items appear quickly and fill in by themselves while you already watch or read.

Ready is decided once by the Engine and is the only gate; no screen has its own rule. It is sticky: once an item is Ready, matching it again (a rematch, a refresh) does not make it disappear. It leaves only on a negative outcome: it lands in Needs Review, finds no match or fails, is rejected, loses its file, or loses its place in its show or album. When it is Ready again it returns without being announced a second time.

An item that is not Ready appears only on the live ingestion page (Settings > Libraries), which says what each one is still waiting for. Each item appears on its own as soon as it is Ready; nothing waits for the rest of a batch. "Added" means Ready: the "added" notice and the Recently Added entry happen at that moment, once per item.

## Delete from library

Delete from library is the one way to remove a title from the library. It is for administrators on a desktop with a fine, hover-capable pointer (`EditorDeviceRule.CanEdit`), and the Engine checks every call. It appears in the places below and nowhere else:

- **Detail page.** **Delete from library…** is the last entry in the page's **...** menu, in red below a divider, and does not depend on the editor. It is offered for single titles such as a movie, an episode, a book, an audiobook or a comic issue. Whole groups have their own entries, described under "Delete a show, season, album or Series" below.

If someone else removes a title while you have it open, the page stays open with a notice that the title is no longer in the library, and the editor, if open, closes. Anything you had typed in a field is kept on the notice with a **Copy** button, so it is not lost.
- **The editor.** The red **Delete** at the left of the editor's footer deletes the item being edited (a whole show, season, album or Series included); ticked rows in the supporting-files browser delete too. One "Are you sure?" box names what goes, how many files, the space freed and any hand-picked collection affected, and says plainly that removed files cannot be recovered (or go to the Recycle bin when that media type has it on). Confirming deletes at once and does not wait for Save changes. Files in a read-only folder are never deleted: the item leaves the Library and is excluded from scans. Unsaved changes on a deleted item are dropped, changes on other items stay waiting, and the editor moves to the parent when its own item is deleted (or closes when it has none).
- **Listen song table.** Each song row has a Delete icon, and selecting songs shows "N selected", **Delete…** and **Clear**. Phones, touch-only devices and anyone who is not an administrator see neither.
- **Settings > Libraries > Excluded files.** The files that stayed on disk, each with **Add back**.
- **Needs Review.** A file the Engine could not identify has **Remove from library** in red; it opens the same dialog for that file.

The dialog first checks what will happen ("Checking what will be deleted…") and then shows:

- **Files**: every media file and sidecar, with its folder and size, each marked as deleted from disk or as staying where it is (in a read-only folder, the original of a merge, or a file that is protected or cannot be removed). Files that stay are excluded from future scans.
- **Hand-picked collections**: each collection the title is in that someone made by hand, with a note that the title is taken out of it. A hand-picked collection left with nothing in it is deleted too, and the dialog names it. Automatic collections are never listed.
- **Progress**: how many people have progress on the title. The count is shown, not their names, and the progress is removed.
- **Space freed**, and "Files deleted from disk can't be recovered." when at least one file will be deleted.

The red button reads **Delete from library and disk** when at least one file will be deleted, and **Delete from library** when every file stays. Cancel has the focus when the dialog opens, and Escape cancels. Collections are sorted A to Z, and after five the list ends with "and N more". The dialog warns when processing for the title will be stopped. If the plan has changed since the dialog opened, the dialog shows the updated list and nothing is deleted.

Library media has no recycle bin. Films, TV, books, audiobooks, comics and music can be downloaded again, so deletion is immediate after the confirmation. A single edition or file, and personal items in View, cannot be deleted this way.

### Delete a show, season, album or Series

An administrator on a desktop can remove a whole group in one go. The entries sit last, in red, in the "..." menu of the group's own page:

- **Delete show…** on a TV show page, which deletes every owned season and episode.
- **Delete Season 2…** on the same page while that season is selected (a season has no page of its own). It sits above Delete show….
- **Delete album…** on an album page, which deletes every owned track.
- **Delete Series…** on a book or comic Series page, which deletes the Series and every owned book or comic in it.

It is the same dialog, preview, safety rules and clean-up as one title, applied to every owned title in the group. Titles the library does not own are never counted or deleted ("Season 3: 6 of 10 episodes owned"). The heading names the group ("Delete "The Expanse", Season 2 from your library?"). Up to twelve titles are listed by name; above that the dialog shows totals by kind ("2 seasons · 38 episodes", "14 books"). The red button counts what goes: **Delete 10 episodes and 10 files**, or **Delete 14 books** when every file stays on disk. Deleting the last season of a show also removes the show, and the dialog says so first.

The biggest deletes also ask for the group's name. When files will be removed for good (not with the Recycle bin on, not when every file stays on disk) and the delete is a whole show, a whole Series, more than 25 titles or more than 50 GB, the dialog shows "Type **The Expanse** to confirm" and the red button stays off until the typed name matches (capitals and extra spaces are ignored). A season asks for "Season 2". The 25 titles and 50 GB limits are settings in the Engine's `maintenance.json`.

The Engine deletes the whole group as one action under one preview fingerprint. If anything changed since the dialog opened, nothing is deleted and the dialog refreshes. A file that cannot be removed stays on disk while the rest is deleted, and one activity entry names the group, the number of titles and files, and the space freed. Other open windows drop the group without a reload.

## Recycle bin

Library types with the Recycle bin switched on keep deleted files in the bin until they are put back or removed for good. A small **Recycle bin · 38 items · 24 GB** entry sits at the foot of a library page when the bin holds something for that type, and **Recycle bin** is in the profile menu for everyone except Kids profiles. The bin opens narrowed to the page you came from, with **Show all** one tap away.

Administrators see every item with who deleted it, when, and how many days it has left, and can **Put back**, **Delete now** or **Empty recycle bin** (always after a confirmation with the size). Other adult profiles see the items and can **Ask to keep**, which tells the administrators. Anything a profile may not see is one line, "Other media: 12 items, 9.1 GB", with no names or kinds, and a Kids profile sees only the totals.

The bin empties itself after 90 days (the number of days is `recycle_bin_retention_days` in `maintenance.json`; 0 means never). Administrators are told when items are within 7 days of leaving, with a card on Overview, and can **Review** or **Keep them**.

People's progress, bookmarks and listening history on a deleted file are kept, by the file's fingerprint, while it waits in the bin. Put the file back and let the library find it, and each person picks up where they left off; once the file leaves the bin for good, so does the kept progress. The delete confirmation says how many people have progress.

## Universe page

A Universe has its own page, `/universe/{qid}`: a page to read, not a management screen. It is not a menu item and the phone dock does not change.

**Finding it.** An item page shows the **Universe icon**, a small galaxy (about 84 px on desktop, 64 px on a phone), at the top right of the landscape background, or on the cover on a phone. It is a square icon button with the tooltip "Open the {name} Universe" (a name that already ends in "Universe" is not given a second one, and one that starts with "The" drops it: "Open the Expanse Universe"). On hover or keyboard focus the galaxy's two bands flow round the core in opposite directions and the four stars twinkle, and on a mouse a small card shows the Universe name, how many Series and Works the Library holds, and what the page offers; none of that moves with reduced motion, and none on touch. It replaces the old "Part of the … Universe" text line. It shows whenever the profile can open the Universe, **including a Universe the Library holds a single Series of**; it is hidden for a title with no Universe, while the Universe is still being built, and for a profile that cannot see it (Kids, private), and it is not drawn on list tiles. Collections lists Universes in their own **Universes** row at the top of the default view (and in the **Universes** group), but only those of two or more Series; a Universe tile opens the page (a Universe without a Wikidata identity keeps the ordinary collection page). Search results for a Universe open it. The Universe details route (`/details/universe/{id}`) has one main button, **Open Universe**. In the editor, Match & Identity shows a read-only "Universe: {name}" row with **Open Universe**.

**Sections, in order, each hidden when empty:**
1. **Hero**: the Universe's name, a stack of covers from its first Series, and one line of totals: "3 Series · 14 Works · 2 formats" (singular and plural are respected; a Universe made only of TV shows says "shows"; standalone works count toward the Work and format totals, and a Universe whose only visible works are standalone still opens). A small "Enrichment running" indicator, blue like the menu indicator, shows while the Engine is still learning about its works.
2. **Series**: one shelf per Series (or show) in the order the Engine gives it, numbered as the source gives it (decimals kept). "Also in this world" sits under its Series. Entries the Library does not own appear as dashed "Not in library" placeholders in their place only when the profile chose to see missing entries for that Series (its own choice, otherwise the media default); otherwise they are not drawn and the Series shows a quiet "N missing" count.
3. **Standalone**: works that belong to the Universe but to no Series, as one shelf.
4. **Connections**, evidence only, every row naming where it came from ("Source: Wikidata", or the approved lore source's name such as "Sources: Wikidata, Dune Wiki"): Cast (headshot, name, "as Character, Character"; a row opens **Also appears in**: every owned Work that shares one of that person's characters, then more of the person's own Work in the Library, each linking onward; the person's page is one link away from there), Adaptations ("Title — Based on Source", only real based-on, derivative or inspired-by links) and Characters (a read-only list: name, "In 3 of your works" and its source, the characters seen in the most works first; a row opens **Also appears in** for that character; the first 12 show with a "Show all" toggle that stays in place as "Show fewer", at most 60 are listed with "Showing the 60 characters seen in the most works" when there are more; no portraits, no relationship graph, no character page). Spoiler respect, one rule for every place a character is named (Characters, Cast, **Also appears in**, the explorer graph): with your spoiler level on **Up to where I am** (the default) a character appears only once you have reached them. A character first seen in an episode you own that is later than the furthest episode you have started is left out, and one honest line under the list says "3 characters hidden to avoid spoilers"; a Cast row keeps the person and drops only those characters' names (so the row has no "as ..." when none remain); **Also appears in** for such a character says it is hidden to avoid spoilers and lists nothing. A character the Library cannot place within what you have watched or read, because it appears only in a show, film or book you have not started, or somewhere within a title with no episode to place it by (books and audiobooks never have chapter data), is listed last with its name, source and a quiet "No plot details" tag only: no work count and no description. It still opens **Also appears in**, which lists Works rather than plot. A character you have met in any one title it appears in (a film you finished, an episode you have reached) is shown, even when another title first sees them later. A character marked as a spoiler for a work you have not finished (or do not own) is hidden. With the level on **Everything** nothing is held back. You choose the level yourself in Settings, Preferences, Playback, **Spoilers** (Up to where I am / Everything); nobody else, not even a household administrator, can change it for you. The same rule covers every other place a character is named (Shy's decision, 2026-10-20): the "as Character" lines on a title's cast list and on a person's page, and the people search, which neither matches nor names ("Plays ...") a character you have not reached. The actor always still shows, just without the hidden character names. A title you have finished shows everyone in it, even a character the Library cannot place at an episode. The admin-only Universe editor is a correction tool and is not filtered. Lists sit side by side as room allows on tablet and desktop, stacked on a phone. Desktop only, an **Open the explorer** link goes to `/universe/{qid}/explore` for paths and the family tree; the explorer's adaptation tree likewise lists only real adaptation links, each with its source, and says plainly when there are none. With nothing recorded and enrichment not finished it says "Still learning about this Universe" with a quiet note such as "Details found for 6 of 14 works"; with nothing recorded and enrichment finished it says "No characters, cast or adaptations recorded for this Universe yet." It never shows sample data. For administrators, when the Fandom lore plugin is off, a quiet "Add Fandom details" link goes to Settings, Plugins.

**States.** Loading shows skeletons for the hero and Series. If the Engine cannot be reached the page says "Can't reach the Engine" with Retry. A Universe the profile cannot see (or that does not exist) says "This Universe isn't available." The page re-reads itself at most once every ten seconds while enrichment events arrive.

**Editing.** Desktop administrators who may edit the Universe see **Edit** in the hero. It opens the Universe in the universal editor, the same pop-over used for every item, with the Universe's kinds across the top; saving a change refreshes the page. Phones, tablets, TVs and everyone else see no Edit control. TV: cards and links are focusable in order with a visible focus ring and nothing is hover-only. Phone: no sideways page scroll, 16 px gutters, 44 px targets.

**Helper apps.** The Android, Apple and TV web apps show the same hero line, Series (in the same order) with "Also in this world" beneath each, and read-only Cast and Adaptations lists from `GET /api/v1/universe/{qid}`, with no graph, Standalone shelf, missing placeholders or editing; Roku lists the Universe's Series only. Details: [Native clients](native-clients.md#collections-and-universes-in-apps).

## Phone companion

The companion is a read-only screen for following a story on a phone or tablet while something plays elsewhere, or while you read, at `/companion` and `/companion/{workId}`. It is offered on phones and tablets only: on a desktop or TV the title menu item and the Home card are hidden and the page says it is for phones and tablets (`CompanionDeviceRule`). It opens with one plain sentence saying what it is, with an (i) tooltip on how to use it. It is not a menu item and the phone dock does not change. It never edits anything; Editing Mode stays desktop-only.

**Getting there.** Home shows a **Now playing** card above the shelves only while the active profile is playing or has paused something on one of its devices ("Lanterns · S1E2, on Living Room TV") with a **Companion** button; with one device the button follows it, with several the card says "and 1 more" and the button opens the companion to choose. Nothing playing means no card. Every title's "..." menu (film, episode, book, audiobook, comic issue) has a **Companion** item that opens `/companion/{workId}`: it holds that title and opens the episode or chapter list so you can pick where you are. On a phone the "..." menu is the bottom sheet. Only the profile's own devices are ever listed; what someone else in the house is playing is never shown.

**The screen**, one column on a phone and 44 px targets throughout: the title and your place ("Season 1 · Episode 2", "Chapter 12: The Gate", "Film"), whether you are following a device ("Following Living Room TV") or holding a place you set; a small **Up to here / Everything** switch (the profile's own Spoilers preference, the same one as Settings, Preferences, Playback, so changing it in either place changes both); buttons to open the item page and its Universe; the synopsis; **Who's here** (characters up to your place, with source and performer, judged by the same spoiler rule as the Universe page; a character the Library cannot place is named with "No plot details"; characters held back are counted in one honest line); **Where you've seen them** (characters that also appear in other Works you own; a row opens **Also appears in**); **Previously** (the last episode you own before this one, with its synopsis); and a **Change episode / Change chapter / Set my place** button that opens the picker. Following refreshes about every five seconds; a place you set never changes by itself. When the followed device stops, the screen keeps where you were and says "Playback stopped."

**Honest limits.** A Work with no character details says "No character details for this one yet." (with the cast and synopsis it does have); a Work whose details exist but are all ahead of you says "Characters show up here as you reach them."; a missing synopsis says "No synopsis for this one yet."; books and audiobooks have no chapter-level source, so their characters show without plot details, and the screen says why. A book without a table of contents gets a chapter-number stepper instead of a list. A film is held as a whole (no scene data), and characters in a film you are watching are named without plot details until you have finished it. Nothing is ever invented to fill a gap. Characters never appear on item pages (they live on the Universe page, the companion and Also appears in).

## Series rails and app shell

Canonical book, comic, and movie series containers show their sequence rail directly on Overview. Source numbering stays above each cover (decimal positions such as 1.5 are shown as the source gives them; a member with no position shows a dot, not a made-up number), connectors appear behind number nodes only between members that sit next to each other in the Engine's order, and the current item uses a stronger purple frame glow without `This book`, `This movie`, or `Up next` labels. Only main-sequence members are numbered and counted in "x of y". Supplementary, collected-content and broader-context members of the same Series appear in a separate **Also in this world** row under the rail, on the Series page and on the item page, and are never numbered with the run. A book Series, an audiobook Series and a TV show that share a name are separate Series. Completion remains a separate check state, and `aria-current` preserves accessible current-item context. Missing-item visibility inherits its media default from `config/ui/library-preferences.json`; the database stores only explicit profile-and-series overrides, which can be removed to restore config inheritance.

A standalone work (no collection, no series value and no parent) never shows a series or sequence panel on its details page. Opening any details page from a browse surface starts at the top, and Back restores the previous page's scroll position.

`MainLayout` exposes My List as the active profile's saved shortlist and delegates account actions to `TopNavAccountMenu`. The profile menu lists the other people first (Netflix order), then Manage profiles (administrators, never inside Kids), Profile, Settings and Help, then the Vault quick check, plugin entries and Sign out; Needs Review is reached from Settings, and a Kid's profile keeps a direct My activity link because it cannot reach Settings. `SystemActivityIndicator` uses `ShellActivityState` to combine ingestion (the Engine's single queue snapshot), AI download/parsing, enrichment and durable-operation activity into one circular progress surface with exactly two states: blue (`--tl-accent-secondary`) while the Library is working and green (`--tl-status-success`) when it is idle, with no amber or other state. Playback does not light it (it is not Library work), and a queue that is only waiting for a missing tool keeps the indicator green, though missing tools and Needs Review items also appear in the administrator banner. It shows blue only while the Engine's snapshot is under 15 seconds old and the live connection is up. The tooltip uses the live page's words ("Found 508 files · 120 of 508 done · About 4 min left", "Found 508 files · 120 of 508 done · Running for 4 min" when the Engine has no reliable estimate, "Waiting for media tools · 3 files are waiting" while tools are missing, or "Library is up to date · Last activity 3 minutes ago" when idle), the state is also written as text for assistive technology, and a click opens the live Ingestion page. The account menu offers **Profile** (the person's Overview, or a Kids profile's own pages at `/my-profile`), shows each person's avatar beside their name in the profile switcher, offers Manage profiles to administrators, and offers Sign out for the current session, including local accounts. Below 600 px (the phone form factor) navigation is the three-tab floating dock, the hamburger menu and the section chips described under *Phone navigation* below. ## My List page

My List is the personal page (it replaces the older My Library page and the separate My List grids, which showed the same saved titles in three places). Route `/my-list`; the heading and the browser tab title say My List. The top-bar bookmark and the phone dock's My List tab both open it. The older addresses `/my-library`, `/for-me?view=my-list` and `/all/personal/my-list` are removed, with no redirects.

Top to bottom:

1. **Profile header.** Avatar and profile name, with links to **Activity**, **Liked**, **Switch profile** (only when the household has more than one profile) and **Settings** (never for Kids). No account editing.
2. **Downloads card.** A link to `/downloads`, shown only where downloads work (the Tuvima app). It is not rendered at all in the browser.
3. **Continue.** Every started title in one row; hidden when nothing is in progress. Source: `GET /display/continue`.
4. **The list.** Every saved title as the standard tile grid (albums and playlists square at poster height), 48 at a time, loading more on scroll. Source: the Engine's My List grid read, which carries the kind counts and the order in one answer so large lists stay fast.

**Kinds.** A row of chips: All, then only the kinds the profile has saved, each with its count (Movies, TV shows, Books, Comics, Audiobooks, Albums, Collections, Playlists). The chosen kind is kept in the address (`?type=`), so Back and a shared link return to it. The row is hidden when the list holds one kind.

**Sort.** Date added (the default, newest first), Title, Release year. The choice is remembered for the profile on every device and applies inside a kind. There is no hand ordering.

**Removing.** A cover's "..." menu has Remove from My List. **Select** turns covers into tick targets with a bar (count, Remove from My List, Cancel; fixed above the dock on phones, where Select lives in the page's "..." menu). Neither asks for confirmation: an **Undo** line stays for about ten seconds and restores the titles with their original saved dates, so the order does not change. No toasts.

**Finished titles** stay on the list. The page's "..." menu has **Remove finished**, which takes off every saved title the profile has completed (a show only when every owned episode is complete); it reads **Nothing finished yet** when there are none.

**States.** Loading shows skeleton covers. An empty list says how to add titles (the plus) with a Browse Home button. A failed read shows "My List could not load" with Try again; a failed change shows an alert in place and leaves the list as it was. With no active profile the page asks the person to choose one. A title the profile can no longer see (hidden library, Kid's level, deleted file) is left out of the page and the counts but is not removed from storage, so it returns if access returns.

**Home row.** Home shows a **My List** shelf directly under Continue with the 20 newest saves, opening this page; it is hidden when the list is empty.

Kid's profiles get the same page, narrowed by the Engine's existing permission and content rules (every read is profile-scoped). Phones get everything desktops do, including Select and Remove finished. TV has no Select.

**Personal pages resolve ids directly.** For Me turns the saved, liked and favorite ids into tiles with `POST /api/v1/display/items` (`PersonalTileIndex.LookupAsync`), not by reading the Read, Watch and Listen lane browse pages, so an item is never missing just because it sits past a lane page's size. The Dashboard sends at most 200 ids per call (the Engine's cap) and splits a longer list into several calls, one page back. The Engine answers from the same authorized projection as the lane pages, so the profile's Kid levels and the profile's lanes and hidden libraries apply exactly as they do on a lane page; an id the profile cannot see, a Collection or Playlist id (those come from the Collection catalogue and Playlist reads) and an id that does not exist all simply have no card. Documents are never returned, so a profile that may not see them cannot learn that one exists, and the Dashboard still hides any Document tile it is handed. Collections and Playlists keep their own reads. If the lookup or the recent-activity read fails, that section is left out and the quiet notice appears, rather than the row showing as empty.

## TV episode routing and heroes

TV episodes are children of the show detail page, organized by season. A combined file (one file, several episodes) is named with its episode run when the organiser renames files, for example `s01e01-e02`; every episode it covers is shown as owned. Each owned
episode has a show-scoped detail route opened from its still. Continue surfaces
retain the episode still and playback target, identify it with compact copy such
as `Continue · S5 E1`, and use actions such as `Resume S5 E1`. Episode detail
heroes retain that episode's still, synopsis, and genre. Root show heroes report
owned episodes and target either the in-progress episode or the first owned episode.
An unstarted show may retain the series hero while showing that episode's facts;
after progress, the hero switches to the episode still and `Sx Ey` description.
Short provider show copy appears under the owned summary in a separated Series
Description block. Detail heroes use one left-aligned column for written identity,
logos, compact facts, actions, and description, and show at most two linked genres
on their own non-wrapping line. Movie heroes
use the movie description without a synopsis heading. The watch utility row does
not repeat a Show details action.

### Seasons open inside their show

A season has no page of its own. Opening one, from any address, shows its show's page
with that season selected in the season control and that season's episodes listed;
it never reads as "not found" while the show and at least one of the season's episodes
are visible to the profile. The address is the show's with the season added
(`/details/tvshow/{show}?season={season}`); `/details/tvseason/{season}` resolves to the
same page. The chosen season is part of the address, so reloading keeps it, and the
season control and episode list behave the same on phones, tablets and desktops. A
season whose episodes the active profile cannot see reads as not found, like any other
item the profile may not see.

### No shareable links; titles open over the current page; the tab is remembered

A library is private, so detail pages have no shareable links and no link-copy action anywhere (not in the More menu of a detail page, the Home hero or the Listen page). Links between items inside the app use the item's ID (`/details/{type}/{id}`). Target behaviour, built with the detail overlay: a title opens as an overlay over the page you were on; Back, the close button and Esc dismiss it and return to the page underneath; reloading while a title is open reopens that same title over Home so you keep your place.

The tab you choose on a detail page (and, on a show, the season) is written into the page address as `tab` and `season` by replacing the current history entry, so it never adds a Back step. Reloading, or coming back to the page, reopens that tab and season; a `tab` value that does not exist or is hidden for that title opens the default tab. Choosing a tab or season never reloads the page from the Engine, and a personal action such as marking something watched keeps the chosen tab and season.

### One file, several episodes: the editor picker

When one video file holds several episodes of a season, the editor's Matching tab
shows a **This file covers** picker for that episode's file. Each episode of the
season is a tappable chip (`E2 · Title`); the file's own episode is always ticked and
locked, episodes that already have their own file are locked, and a file covers at
most six episodes, all within one season. **Save episodes** replaces the list in one
step, answers the file's pending combined-media Review item, and records a History
entry; saving the same list again changes nothing. Single-episode files see no change
beyond the picker staying out of the way when the season has one episode. On phones
the chips stack full width with 44px touch targets and the actions stay reachable
inside the modal.

### Whole miniseries moved to TV

When a Movies item turns out to be a TMDB miniseries and **Move to TV** is used from
its "Found as a TV title" review card, the show appears under TV and the file covers
every part TMDB lists, for example Season 1, Episodes 1 to 3, all shown as owned. The
file is attached to the first episode, the others carry the "Same file as Episode 1"
chip, and the file on disk is never moved or renamed. The same safety rules as any
combined file apply: at most six parts, all in one season, numbered as an unbroken
run from Episode 1. Only when TMDB's parts cannot be listed that clearly (more than
six, several seasons, a gap, or the episode list could not be fetched) is the file
filed as a special instead (Specials, Season 0, Episode 1), the other episodes show
as not owned, and the move's message and the item's History say why. The range can
be corrected later with the **This file covers** picker.

## TV episode context and personal status (September 2026)

TV detail uses one profile-aware continuation policy: unstarted/reset shows retain series artwork, active shows use the current or next owned episode still, and explicit episode details stay episode-scoped. Completed owned runs offer rewatch with series artwork. Episodes use short episode synopsis text, never Wikipedia extracts. More exposes media-appropriate personal completion/reset, history, Undo, Continue visibility, and applicable queue/playlist/collection utilities independently of metadata-edit permission. Revision checks prevent stale progress writes from reversing a reset; bookmarks, genuine consumption history, and music play counts are preserved.

### Long seasons

A show with up to 100 episodes lists every season and episode at once. (The Dashboard asks for the paged form; clients that do not ask still receive the whole list.) A longer show lists every season but loads one season's episodes at a time, 50 per window, starting at the window that holds the episode being watched. Next-episode playback and the player's episode picker ask the Engine for the whole list, so they cross season and window boundaries.

When one video file holds several episodes, each covered episode in a season strip carries a quiet "Same file as Episode N" chip under its tile. The chip data arrives with the episode item (one batched lookup per page, no per-card requests). When the other episode is in the visible strip the chip is a 44px button that highlights it (press again to clear); otherwise it is plain text. It wraps on phone widths, follows the dark theme and needs no motion. Files without combined episodes look exactly as before.

One photo, video or document has Details, Files and History; a gallery has Details, Contents, Artwork and History. Nothing is saved until **Save changes**, which shows **Review changes** first and then saves everything as one step, all or none; if any item is refused, the reason names it and nothing changes. Delete is the one exception: it asks "Are you sure?" once and then happens at once (to the Recycle bin; a gallery's photos stay in the library), and an item in a Read-only folder shows the reason instead. Edits are kept in Library and never written into the files. A group offers only the shared fields (tags, people, date taken or shifted, place for photos and videos; tags, correspondent, type and date for documents); description, title, archive number, notes and custom fields are one-item fields; fields that differ say "Mixed, leave unchanged". Opened from a gallery, the rail reads "Gallery > Photo" and steps through the gallery's photos while keeping what was staged on the others. The viewer's side panel only shows facts and has one **Edit** button; there are no per-field pencils or per-field saves. Favorite, Hide, Archive, Add to gallery and New gallery stay one-click and confirm in the slim ribbon, never in a toast. A gallery cover is chosen by clicking a picture to make it Preferred, or Automatic. Editing is desktop-only (a fine, hover-capable pointer); phones, tablets and TVs never show Edit.

**Documents Inbox and auto-filing rules.** The first choice in the Documents filter rail is **Inbox** with a count ("12 to file"); next is **All documents**, which stays the default, and in the person's own space a third choice, **Trash**, lists documents moved to the trash (Select works in all three). The View menu's Documents tab shows the person's own Inbox count as a badge only when it is above zero. In Inbox mode each result is an Inbox card: thumbnail, title, detected date ("Detected …", or "File date …" when no date was found in the words and the file's time is shown instead), and chips for the correspondent, type, suggested tags and, when a custom field named Amount exists, the suggested amount. A value a rule already filled in is shown instead of the suggestion. **File** accepts the card in one tap (Enter does the same on a computer when the card is focused) and sends exactly what the card shows (the suggestions as displayed, minus any cleared or switched off, plus typed values), so a suggestion that arrives while the card is open is never filed unseen; tapping the correspondent or type chip opens a small dialog to pick or type another name or clear it, tapping a tag chip switches it off or on, and "Add tag" adds one; **File without changes** only marks it filed. Suggestions are shown, never applied silently, except by a rule the person made. Filing works on phones in the person's own space; television and other people's spaces are read-only. Documents in the Shared Library show in its Inbox to everyone who can open it. A household administrator or the server administrator files them with the same **File** and **File without changes** actions, so a Shared Inbox can be emptied; everyone else, including child profiles, sees those cards read-only with the note "Filed by a household administrator". Bulk editing skips Shared documents with a reason on each, except **Mark filed**, which administrators can use on them. An empty Inbox says "Your Inbox is empty." A document is filed on arrival only when a matching rule files automatically; other matching rules leave it in the Inbox with their values filled in. Rules are made on the **Auto-filing rules** page (`/view/documents/rules`, the button in the Documents header on computers and tablets; everyone with Documents reaches their own, phones and tablets see a read-only list with "Edit on a computer", televisions get a plain "not available here" page) and the same list also sits in Settings > Libraries > View for administrators: a plain list (name, a sentence saying what it does, on/off switch, up and down buttons for the running order, edit, delete), "Add rule" and "Apply rules to existing documents", with an editor dialog (name, enabled, all/any, conditions: words contain, file name contains, folder contains, came from a source, file kind; actions: add tag, set correspondent, set type, set field, file automatically; "Test on my documents" lists up to ten matches). On phones and tablets the rule list is read-only with "Edit on a computer". Rules never replace a value a person set, including one they cleared. Document words, notes and amounts appear only on the Documents page, never in Home, global Search or Recommendations. Home's Recently Added lists photos and videos from View only: a document's title, file name or id never appears there, for anyone, and Places and People discovery skip documents too. See [How documents are filed](../explanation/how-documents-are-filed.md).

### Photos and Personal videos: Import, filing, duplicates and Tidy (October 2026)

Photos and Personal videos have one Import area, outside every library folder, shown with its path, how many files are waiting, **Scan now** and **Add files** on the library's Folders tab. New files are filed as `Year/Year-Month-Day/original name` by the day they were taken; the layout is editable on File handling and applies to files filed later. Companion files (RAW with JPEG and sidecar, Live Photo with video) stay together under one base name. An exact copy is skipped and counted ("3 already in your library"); a file that cannot be used is refused with a plain reason, listed on the library's Activity tab (counts and reasons only, never file names for a private library), kept in the Rejected area for 30 days and then removed. Folders tab rows for managed folders offer **Tidy this folder by date** (a confirmation says how many files are renamed in place and that new files wait until it finishes) and a nightly schedule. File handling shows **Duplicates**: groups of exact copies in the Shared Library or the administrator's own library, with **Keep this one** (the others go to Trash) and **Leave them**. The Needs review link on a library opens Needs Review narrowed to that library.

### View Trash and Empty Trash

Photos' **Trash** filter and Documents' **Trash** (in the person's own space only) start with a header: "Items in Trash stay on your disk until you empty Trash.", then the size line ("128 items · 4.2 GB") on every device. On a computer (`EditorDeviceRule.CanEdit`) with something in Trash, a red **Empty Trash…** button sits beside it. On a phone or tablet the header shows "Empty Trash on a computer." instead, with no button. Empty Trash empties the whole Trash, photos and documents together, one Trash per Personal Space, and its confirmation dialog lists photos, videos and documents separately. Empty Trash appears only in the person's own space (Mine), so Shared Library and other people's spaces never show it. Items in a read-only (linked) folder never show **Move to Trash**, and a mixed selection says how many were left out. Phones keep **Move to Trash** and **Restore**.

## Settings

Settings opens at `/settings` with one flat menu: every row is a page, never a folder that opens into more rows. A page that has several views shows them as a row of tabs under its title, in the same tab style as the rest of the app, and each tab has its own address so Back, reload and a shared link all land on the same tab. The menu keeps its rows in three groups, in this order (a group shows only for the people allowed to see it):

- **Personal** (everyone): **Profile** (tabs Overview, Activity, Taste, Linked accounts, Import; the person's own page, also where the bare `/settings` address and the profile menu's *My profile* go, at `/settings/overview`), **Manage Profiles** (administrators see the household's people; everyone else lands on their own profile, whose own tabs are Profile, Preferences and Access & safety, where Preferences has a switch between Watching, Listening, Reading and Home, one section at a time) and **Security & Devices** (tabs Sign-in and Devices).
- **Library** (server administrators): **Libraries** (tabs Libraries, Activity, Needs Review with its count, Matching, Rules; one status line above the table replaces the old overview, and Recently Added is folded into Activity) and **Playback** (tabs Quality, Advanced).
- **Server** (server administrators): **General**, **Users & Households** (tabs Users, Households, Advanced), **Network & Remote Access** (tabs Home network, Remote access, Advanced), **Tools**, **Local AI** (tabs Status, Models, Advanced), **Plugins**, **Integrations**, **Backups** (tabs Backups, Destinations, Advanced) and **System** (tabs Health, Scheduled tasks, Logs, Advanced).
- **Developer Tools** (server administrators, development builds only): Provider Tester, Enrichment Tester, Development data.

**Advanced is a tab, never a page.** Every page with advanced settings has the same last tab, named **Advanced**, at the right end of the tab row, drawn with a small gear icon and an amber tint instead of the purple accent, with one shared warning line at its top: "These settings are for experienced administrators. Everyday use never needs them." Nothing advanced appears outside it. **General** (`/settings/general`, no tabs) has four panels: the Library's name, its `.local` address (worked out from the name, with a **Change** link), its numeric address with **Copy**, the QR code and the app links; Language and region; and Notifications (with the email test). **Tools** (`/settings/tools`) lists every tool in the same row as first-run setup (name and version, a one-line description, a status of Ready, Not installed, Downloading or Broken, and one action such as Install, Locate, Run installer or Cancel) under a summary line such as "5 tools detected · 4 ready · 1 needs setup". Buttons have three levels: Primary (filled, one per screen), Secondary (outlined) and Quiet (text only, for the least important action such as Skip or Not now).

A Kids profile sees **Profile** only, and anything else in Settings sends it Home. Phones and tablets do not list the Developer group, and pages that are desktop-only stay out of the phone page selector. The page name and tab are both in the browser title and the breadcrumb ("Settings > Library > Libraries > Needs Review"). Retired addresses are not found and do not redirect: the old `/settings/ingestion`, `/settings/delivery`, `/settings/access` Sign-in Methods and System Configuration addresses no longer exist. These still open the right page and tab: `/settings/preferences`, `/settings/playback`, `/settings/listening`, `/settings/reading` and `/settings/home` open the person's own Preferences tab, `/settings/profile` and `/settings/household` go to Manage Profiles, `/settings/account` goes to Security & Devices, and `/settings/access/applications` goes to Integrations. `/settings/devices`, `/settings/apps` and `/settings/network/apps` no longer exist and do not redirect. **Names used further down this page:** an older heading that says Overview means Profile, Profiles means Manage Profiles, Security means Security & Devices, Library and Ingestion means Libraries, "Users and Sign-in" means Users & Households (its Sign-in Methods content is now the Advanced tab), Streaming and Delivery mean Playback, "Tools and requirements" means Tools, Network and Remote Access means Network & Remote Access, Backup and recovery means Backups, and Logs and Scheduled tasks mean tabs of System.

**Settings > Overview.** `/settings/overview` is the person's own page and the first Personal entry (a Kid's profile never reaches Settings; it has the same page at `/my-profile`, with Overview, Activity and Taste tabs, no Edit profile and no Apps button). It shows the signed-in profile only, worked out on this machine from their own history with nothing invented: a banner with their avatar, name, *Joined {Month Year}*, **Edit profile** and a small phone icon (*Apps*); then a strip of four tiles: *In progress*, *Finished* (last 30 days), *Hours enjoyed* (all time) and *Days active* (last 30 days). "Last 30 days" is a rolling window ending today, counted in UTC days, so it always moves. **Continue enjoying** shows up to three cards (one each for Reading, Watching and Listening) for what is part way through. **Recent activity** is a timeline under *Today*, *Yesterday* and dated headings, each row with its time, cover, title, a progress figure or a green *Finished* mark and a **...** menu (Open, See all activity); *See all* opens the Activity page. **Your taste** is the *You love ...* sentence with the top genres as chips and a *See your taste* link. **Last 30 days** says how many days the person was active and how many titles they finished, over a calendar heatmap of daily time (hidden for a new profile). A new profile shows zeros and a short invitation instead. There is no streak, no star ratings and no likes. Edit profile opens the profile editor sheet in its self-edit form: a person changes only their own name and picture; the Kids switch, content limit, PIN and icons stay with administrators on the person's page in Profiles. The page works on a phone: the strip wraps two by two and buttons are full width. Photos (View) are not counted or shown. Page: `OverviewPage`; parts: `OverviewStatStrip`, `OverviewContinueEnjoying`, `OverviewTimeline`, `OverviewHeatmap`; wording and maths: `ProfileOverviewPresentation` and `PersonalActivityPresentation`; figures: `GET /api/v1/activity/summary` (including the rolling 30-day finished count, days active and the daily history).

**Settings > Overview > Recent activity.** Every tile is the same height and keeps its natural width by shape (`portrait` for books and comics, `landscape` for episodes and movies, `square` for music and audiobooks). Each shows its artwork, a thin progress bar along the bottom for a watched, read or listened item part way through (the shared `AppArtworkProgress`), a check instead of the bar once it is finished, and no bar for music or My list. Under it is one non-wrapping caption that starts with a verb so it cannot be read as the length of the item: *Watched 52 m*, *Read 1 h 20 m*, *Listened 3 h 12 m* (audiobooks), *Played 1 h 05 m* (music) or *Added to My list*. Titles are not printed. Music is one **music session** tile, a square collage of up to nine album covers (a different nine on each load) whose hover card says how many songs from how many albums. On a screen that can hover, hovering or focusing a tile opens a card built on `AppPopover` with a larger picture, the title, the episode or chapter, when it happened and how long; on a phone or any screen without hover there is no card and a tap opens the item (a music session, or an item whose record no longer points at anything, opens the Activity page, `/settings/overview/activity`). *See all* opens the Activity page too. With nothing yet the strip says *Nothing yet — what you watch, read and listen to shows up here.* The pictures are private to the person, so the browser loads them from the Dashboard route `/engine-activity-artwork/{key}`, which asks the Engine as the signed-in person.

**Settings > Overview > Activity.** `/settings/overview/activity` (the first child of Overview in the menu, before Taste; `/my-activity` is the same page outside Settings for a Kids profile (its Activity tab) and quick access, always the signed-in person's own activity and always in the normal app layout) is the one list of everything the person watched, listened to, read and added to My list, newest first, laid out like the Overview timeline: a purple dot beside each date heading and a **...** menu on every row (Open, and Find this title, which searches the list for that title). Each row shows the time, the picture, the title with its episode (*S2 E4 · Fear*) or artist, what happened (*Watched*, *Listened*, *Read*, *Played* for a music session, *Added to My list*), how long, how far (the percent reached), how it ended (*Finished* or *Stopped*) and the device; a tap opens the item. No IP addresses, sign-in details, ratings or likes appear. Above the list: a search box (title and device; in a scope that includes other people also the person), type (Watched, Listened, Read, Added to My list), a from and to date, outcome (Any, Finished, Not finished), device, and the grouping *By day*, *By week*, *By month* or *By title* (the shared `AppTimelineGroupingControl`, extended with options), with **Show every session** (on in *By title* only) turning the title groups into a flat list. The filters show as removable chips (`AppActiveFilterSummary`); on a phone they sit behind one **Filters** button in a bottom sheet (`BrowseFilterSheet`). *By title* gives one entry per title that expands to its attempts, for example *Started Sep 6, 30 m, stopped at 31% · Sep 9, 12 m, stopped at 43% · Sep 13, finished, 1 h 36 m in total*. A music session is one row with its collage and a **Show songs** button that asks the Engine for that session's songs. A slim **date rail** beside the list (`AppTimelineNavigator`, the one the View photos pages use) shows years opening to months with counts; it follows the filters and the scope, jumping to a period restarts the list at the end of that period (a chip *From Sep 2026 back* clears it), and on a phone it becomes the *Jump to period* picker. **Load more** pages the list with the Engine's cursor, and the next page also loads by itself as the person scrolls near the end (the button stays for keyboards, remotes and screen readers; after a failed page it stops loading by itself until the person presses Load more again). **Export** downloads a CSV of exactly the current filters through the Dashboard route `/engine-activity-export`, which passes on only the filters (never paging or grouping). An administrator also gets a **Scope** control: a server administrator chooses Me, Everyone, a household or one person; a household administrator chooses Me, a person in their household or the household. Other scopes add a **Person** column, and only a server administrator sees Export for them. The Engine decides what any scope may show, logs a server administrator opening someone else's activity, and asks for the administrator PIN when one is set (the page then shows the PIN pad, and asks again after it is entered). A link such as *View activity* on a person's page opens the page with `?scope=profile:{id}`; a scope the viewer could not choose is ignored. Only a server administrator sees the **...** menu with **Delete history...**: choose whose history and a date, see how many entries would go, type DELETE and confirm; members and household administrators have no delete control. Page: `ActivityPage` (rows: `ActivityRow`); wording and date maths: `ActivityListPresentation`.

**Settings > Overview > Taste.** `/settings/overview/taste` (a child of Overview in the Personal group; the breadcrumb reads Personal, Overview, Taste; Kids see it at `/my-profile/taste`) shows what the Library has learned about a person's taste, all worked out on this machine with no AI: a *You love ...* sentence from the top three genres, a **Genres** donut (the top five and *Other*), a **Formats** bar (movies, TV, books, audiobooks, music and comics in their media colours), **Key tastes** (Genres, Moods and Eras as chips), **How your taste shifts** (titles finished each month, stacked by the genres finished most) and a footer *Based on N finished titles · Last worked out {date}*. Until the Library has learned enough, the page says taste appears after a few finished titles and shows only what is known. On your own taste each key-taste chip has an X that hides it, and each group has an **Add** control (free text, with known values offered as small chips that narrow as you type; an era is a decade such as 1990s). A value you typed shows *Added by you*. Nothing you do rewrites what the Library learned: removals and additions are kept separately and applied when taste is shown, and the Overview's *Your taste* card follows them. Other people's taste is always read-only (no X, no Add). **A Kids profile sees its own taste read-only**: the page hides the X and Add controls and the Engine refuses the edit. A **Scope** filter appears for administrators only: a server administrator can pick Me, Everyone, a household or a person; a household administrator can pick Me, their own household or a person in it. It only offers choices; the Engine decides what each person may see and refuses the rest, and opening someone else's taste is logged (`taste.viewed_others`, once per 30 minutes per actor and scope). A member sees no filter and only their own taste. More like this, Less like this and a mood summary are not shown; they wait for an AI taste plugin. The page works on a phone. Page: `OverviewTastePage` (parts: `TasteDonut`, `TasteFormatMix`, `TasteShifts`); Engine actions: `/api/v1/taste`.

**Settings > Overview > Apps.** `/settings/overview/apps` (no menu row; the breadcrumb reads Personal, Overview, Apps) is what a person needs to connect a phone or TV, and every signed-in person sees it. **Get the app** lists a button for each store that has a link in `app_store_links` (`app_store`, `google_play`, `roku`) in `config/ui/global.json`; all three ship blank, so nothing shows until an app is published. **Server address** shows *At home* and, once a secure outside address is set up, *Away from home*, each with Copy (from `GET /network/app-addresses`; an administrator without an outside address gets a link to Remote Access, anyone else is told to ask the server administrator). The **QR code** holds exactly one address and nothing else, never a key or a sign-in: the outside address when it is set, otherwise the one at home, with a label saying which. Scanning it fills in the app's server field; the person still signs in as usual.

**Settings > Profiles.** Profiles is an editing page for administrators (a server administrator, or a household administrator for their own household); a member never sees it in the menu, and opening `/settings/profiles` as a member lands on their first available page. A member changes their own name, picture and details from Edit profile on their Overview and looks after sign-in under Security. The page is the people list and nothing else: no card or stats for the open profile (those belong to the Overview). One 56 px row per person shows their avatar, name, their sign-in email in a small line under the name (or *No sign-in*; read-only here, because a server administrator attaches and removes sign-ins in Users & Households), badges (*You*, *Household administrator*, *Server administrator*, *Kids*, *PIN*) and an **Edit** button that opens that person's own page at `/settings/profiles/{profileId}`. People are grouped under their household's name; a household administrator sees only their own household, and a server administrator sees every household, their own first. The last row of each group is *Add profile* (it opens the editor sheet and adds to that group's household) while the household has fewer than eight people; at eight the Engine refuses and its sentence is shown as it is. A server administrator also gets **Add household**, which opens the Households page (under Users & Households) with the name box ready. Each group's header carries the household's **What Kids can see** settings (a level each for Watch, Read and Listen and a **Show unrated items** switch; administrators only). There is no "Users in your household" panel: household administrators look after profiles, not users. There is no Switch action anywhere on this page: switching profile happens only in the top-bar profile menu. Kid's profiles never reach Settings: the links are hidden, and a typed address or old link to any `/settings` page sends a Kid's profile to Home. The picker's *Manage profiles* button and the account menu's *Manage profiles* item open this page for administrators (a television keeps managing in place on the picker, which also still honours `?manage=1` and `?add=1`).

**Settings > Users & Households > Households.** `/settings/access/households` is the Households page, a child of Users & Households in the Manage group and open to server administrators only (a household administrator sees neither the menu row nor the page). The old `/settings/profiles/households` address was retired with no redirect. It lists every household with its name and how many people and sign-ins it holds, and offers **Add household** (a name), **Rename** and **Remove** on each. Remove asks first; the Engine refuses while the household still has people, sign-ins or shared photos, and its plain sentence is shown as it is. While the server administrator's own sign-in has no email and password (or passkey), the Engine refuses a second household: the page shows its sentence (*Set an email and password for your own sign-in first. A Library with several households must not be open to anyone who reaches it.*) with a **Set an email and password** button that opens Settings > Security. With one household the Library may still be used without passwords. 

**Settings > Playback.** Playback holds the choices the player does not own. *How you watch.* shows **Resume where you left off**, a **On the progress bar** card with *Show chapter marks on the progress bar* (off for video and on for audiobooks until the person chooses), and, for the person themselves only, **Spoilers**. Each is saved as soon as it changes, and nothing the player does not honour is listed. Autoplay next episode is not here: it is the switch in the footer of the player's Episodes panel (Lessons for a folder video or course). Skip lengths, speed and picture-in-picture are in the player's Playback settings, and subtitle size, background, font and colors are in its Subtitle appearance panel. Below those, the **Subtitles & audio** group (the `#subtitles` anchor, which Settings > Profiles > a person > Subtitles opens) sets Audio language, Subtitle language (a common-language list; a saved language that is not in it stays selectable), an optional second and third subtitle language, Lyrics language and Show subtitles (*Auto (only when audio is not my language)*, *Always* or *Off*). An administrator can open `/settings/playback?profile={id}` to edit another household profile; the page then reads *How (name) watches.* with that profile's avatar, a Settings > Profiles > name > Playback breadcrumb and a link back to the profile, and it shows "You can't change this person's settings." with no controls when the Engine refuses. If the Engine cannot be reached when the page opens, it shows "Playback settings could not be loaded." with a Retry button and no controls (it never shows defaults that a change could save over the profile's real choices). Each change re-reads the profile's current settings and changes only that one field before saving, so a change made on another device in the meantime is kept; if that re-read fails, the page shows the error and saves nothing.

**Settings > Listening.** *How you listen.* groups **Skip** (Skip back 10/15/30 s, Skip forward 15/30/60 s, Resume rewind 0/5/10/15/30 s) and **Sleep timer** (one switch, *Offer end of chapter*, which decides whether the audiobook Sleep timer panel offers End of chapter), each saved as soon as it changes. There are no sleep timer default-length rows, and no speed, skip-silence, crossfade or output rows. Administrators open `/settings/listening?profile={id}` for another household profile with the same heading, breadcrumb, back link and refusal message as the Playback page. It loads and saves the same way as Playback: an unreachable Engine shows an error with Retry and no controls, and each change is merged into a fresh read of the profile's settings.

**Settings > Reading.** *How you read.* sets the profile's reading defaults, with a live sample paragraph at the top that follows the chosen theme, typeface, text size and line spacing, then **Look** (Theme: Light, Sepia, Dark or Match app; Typeface: Book's own, Serif or Sans; Text size 12 to 32 px; Line spacing 1.2 to 2.4) and **Layout** (Page width: Narrow, Standard or Wide; Layout: Paginated or Scrolled), each saved as soon as it changes. Administrators open `/settings/reading?profile={id}` for another household profile with the same heading (*How (name) reads.*), breadcrumb, back link and refusal message as the Listening page, and the same load error with Retry and merge-on-save behaviour.

**Settings > Security.** `/settings/security` is short row lists, not a wall of cards. *Account details* starts with **Add a password** (status "Anyone who can reach Tuvima can use this account until you do", opening the Secure your account form) only while the account works only on this computer; the Security entry in the Settings navigation carries an attention dot in that same state until the person chooses Not now on the profile menu card; after that the dot is gone and the page starts with a quiet note ("Your account has no password, so it only works at home. Add one any time below."). The Secure your account form explains that a password is for using the Library away from home. *Account details* then shows **Password** (Set, or "Not set · works on this computer only"), **Email** (the address, or "None · only needed away from home") and, only once a password exists, **Extra protection** (Off, Passkey added, Two-step codes on, or both). Everyone who is signed in gets an *Access* list with **Sign out everywhere else** (asks to confirm, then signs out every other session of their own account and never the one in use, so a member who loses a phone can end its session without an administrator); **Devices** is a child of Security in the Settings menu (`/settings/security/devices`), open to everyone who is signed in except Kids profiles; this page carries no Devices row and no paired-devices table. Each row opens a sheet (a bottom sheet on phones, a dialog from 641 px): Password opens the change-password form, or the Secure your account form when there is no password yet; Email is read-only with "Ask an administrator to change it"; Extra protection stacks Passkeys, Two-step codes, Sign in with another account (only when a provider is set up) and Recovery codes. A third list, *Your photos*, has one row, **Who viewed your photos** ("Server administrators who opened them, newest first"), for everyone who is signed in; it opens a read-only sheet with a Who / Opened / When table (stacked cards on phones), newest first, with times in UTC, 25 at a time with *Show more* up to the latest 100 (a failed *Show more* says *Couldn't load more* and keeps the list). There are no delete or clear controls. The Engine answers only about the signed-in person's own photos (a household administrator also sees views of the household's Shared Library, never another person's personal-space views). With nothing recorded the sheet says *Nobody else has looked at your photos*; if the list cannot be loaded it says so and shows no entries.

**Settings > Users & Households > Users.** A user is a sign-in: an email with its password, passkeys and two-step codes, attached to one profile. The page lists one row per user: the email, the profile it opens, the household, and a small badge only when it matters (*No password*, *Two-step on*, *Disabled*). There is no access editing here: what a person may see and do (administrator roles, libraries, Read, Watch, Listen and View, the Kid's profile switch) is set on their profile, under Settings > Profiles. The page is for server administrators only; everyone else sees "Administrator access is required to manage users." and nothing to manage, and the handlers refuse as well as the buttons. A row's "..." menu offers **Change email**, **Set temporary password**, **Turn off two-step codes** (only when the user has them), and **Delete sign-in**. The toolbar offers **Add a sign-in**. **Add a sign-in** opens the side drawer: choose *A profile without a sign-in* (listed with its household) or *A new person* (a name, a household that already exists or *A new household* with its name, and a **Kid's profile** tick that starts off), an email, and how they sign in the first time, **Send an invitation** (a code, a link and a QR code, shown once) or **Set a temporary password** (shown once). When the Engine refuses (a second household while a server administrator has no password, eight profiles in a household, a profile that already has a sign-in, an email already in use) its sentence is shown as it is and the form stays open. **Change email** saves and says "Their profile and everything in it are unchanged." Management is computer or tablet only; a phone shows the read-only list ("Manage on a computer or tablet.").

**Deleting a sign-in.** Deleting a user removes the sign-in only; the profile and everything in it stay. The drawer asks the Engine first (`GET /access/users/{id}/deletion-preview`) and says what will happen: "Delete the sign-in mom@home.com? Mom's profile and everything in it stay in Household 1. You can attach a new sign-in to it later." When it is the last sign-in that can look after a household that is not the default one, the household goes with its profiles, and the drawer says so: "This is the last sign-in that can look after Household 2. Deleting it removes Household 2 and its profiles: Pat, Sam, Leo, with their progress, bookmarks, lists, photos and videos." Photos and videos, theirs and the household's Shared Library, are deleted with the household and never moved into another household (users spec D18), so there is no photo choice. ## Shared editor and Universe graph rules

- Treat structural media placement, authored-container canonical title/order, and the knowledge graph as separate models. Stage 2 establishes canonical Work identity; bounded Stage 3 enriches grounded entities, qualified graph links, and graph-owned artwork without silently changing authored containers or structural placement.
- **Editing is desktop-only.** One rule, `EditorDeviceRule.CanEdit`, decides: the form factor is desktop AND the browser reports a fine, hover-capable primary pointer (`pointer: fine` and `hover: hover`). Phone, tablet, TV, a native app shell, an unknown form factor and a browser that cannot report its pointer all mean no, so an iPad asking for the desktop site cannot edit while a touch laptop with a mouse can. The browser snapshot (`detectDeviceSnapshot`, refreshed on resize and when a pointer is plugged in or removed) and the server circuit (`DeviceContextService.CanEdit`) feed the same rule, and every Dashboard edit handler refuses when it is false, not only the hidden buttons: the media, collection and gallery launchers return `NotAvailableOnThisDevice` and the page shows "Edit on a computer" in place, Editing Mode will not start, a pencil will not open and a field will not save. No Edit, Correct, Rematch, Edit artwork, New/Edit Gallery, New Collection or New Smart Playlist control renders off the desktop, and a window dragged to tablet width leaves Editing Mode by itself. Phones, tablets, TVs, cars and native apps are consumption-only in the Dashboard: no pencils, no Edit button, no drawer and no Dashboard edit routes. The Engine never sees the device: its permission check still decides who may edit, and it does not block coarse-pointer or touch devices. The phone More sheet may keep one disabled row reading "Edit on a computer" where Edit sat; tablets show nothing about editing, and a menu that held only Edit is removed. Needs Review stays a desktop workflow (phones keep its summary only; Review off the desktop does nothing).
- **Editing (the universal editor, a pop-over).** A person with edit permission, on a device that can edit, chooses **Edit** in an item's "..." menu (on its page, a list row, Recently Added) or **Fix** on a Needs Review row; the editor opens at once as a pop-over over whatever page they were on, which does not change or scroll, and always on the item that was chosen. Top to bottom it has the **header**, a **tab row**, the tab and a fixed **footer**. The header looks like the item: a 96 x 144 cover (a picture only, not a link), the show's or album's name as a plain label, the item's title, one line of facts with its match state, links to its provider records and Wikidata, and a gradient background built from the cover's two main colors (a cover with no colors gets a plain dark window). Under the title is the navigation row: an accent **home button** (the way up to the show, album or Series), a **Season** dropdown (**Section** on a course), and an **item** dropdown listing the episodes (still, code, title, a tick when matched, a warning when not, a purple dot when changes are waiting), then the supporting files that belong there, **All supporting files** when there are more than eight, and a **folder button, Browse files and folders**. A film, a standalone book or a person has no home button or second dropdown (the item dropdown appears only when it has supporting files); an album has home and a track dropdown; a book in a Series has home (the Series) and a book dropdown; a Universe keeps its kind dropdowns. The **tabs** are Details, Artwork, Match and History (a supporting file: Details, Preview and History; a Series: Details and History; administrators also get Bookmarks on a title that plays). There is no Contents tab and no Files tab. An item that is not matched opens on Match with its search filled in from the folder and file names. In Details every provider-filled field is **locked by default** (dimmed, dashed border, a lock in the accent colour beside the label); clicking the lock unlocks it, a changed value is kept on every later provider refresh and shows **Revert**, and the person's own fields (tags, sort title, supporting-file fields) are never locked. **Quick facts** sit beside the form and carry the file's quality, container, size and folder. **Match** opens on the current match, found by its provider ID, with a Why this match table (Strong, Possible or Check with a reason) and **Open source**, **Change match** and **Remove match**; it has two independent sides, **Media providers** and **Wikidata**; a search runs only when the person presses Search or changes a field; **Apply match** is always allowed. There is no "Change placement" and no table lining up every file. Artwork shows a card per role with its versions and Preferred, a large zoomable preview at the role's real shape, file facts, and provider choices filtered by language and size. The footer reads, left to right: a red **Delete**, the file status ("File write-back off: Edits save in Library. Original files stay unchanged." or "File write-back on: Edits save in Library and are written into the file's own tags."), the unsaved count, **Discard** and **Save changes**; a match that will move a file in a Managed folder replaces the status with an amber warning until the person saves or discards. Changes on every item are kept while switching along the header. Nothing is saved until **Save changes**, except Delete; every other change is staged, counted on the footer and listed in **Review changes** (before and after), which saves only on Save. Provider facts are read-only until unlocked. Several ticked rows open the same pop-over ("Editing 4 songs") and offer only the fields that make sense for all of them. After saving, a slim ribbon on the page names each finishing step. Closing with changes asks "Unsaved changes". There is no editing bar, no left tree, no per-field pencil, no edit mode, no drawer and no undo journal; a deep link never opens the editor. Moving a song, episode or audiobook part is always match first; a book, comic or movie is never moved into a Series it does not belong to.
- **A Series is read-only (universal editor v5, item 20).** Edit on a Series (a Library Series, or a book, film, comic or audiobook Series) shows its name and its members in order, locked, with a notice that a Series is made from its members' own matches; picking a member opens that member's editor in the same window. A Series has only Details and History (no Artwork: its tile is always the stack of its members' covers) and its footer has only **Close**. The Engine refuses every write to a Series (fields, pictures, match changes). A Universe takes overrides only (a description override, aliases and extra pictures); its provider facts are read-only and have no unlock.
- **One write-back control.** Whether a save also updates the files on disk is the library's **Save changes into the files** setting in Settings > Libraries (per library, identical for every item type and every way in). The editor never offers a second switch: Review changes shows a small read-only line ("Files will be updated: On" or "Off") with a **Change in Settings** link, and only when the library allows it a **Update the files when this is saved** tick box for that one save. Unticking it saves the changes to the library and leaves the files alone.
- The editor names the item being edited in its header, and the navigation row shows where it sits in its family, so there is always exactly one highlighted editing target. Keep audiobook chapter-title overrides under Chapters. Match information sits in Match and in the header's facts line, never in selector badges; inherited canonical identity needs no status label. Match owns provider identity plus proposed structural placement; preview previous/target paths before Apply. Structural parent moves live in Match, not Details, and Details stays lean (metadata, local fields and sorting). Do not expose Change Type in the normal editor or Review Queue.
- Universe/entity editing uses the same pop-over editor, opened from **Edit** on the Universe page or the Chronicle explorer. Under the title are the Universe's kinds (Characters, Places, Factions, Events, Objects, Species, and Concepts when Fandom Lore is on) as searchable dropdowns with counts and **Add** inside each; a chosen thing has Details, Pictures (main picture, logo, background), Match (Wikidata, or "Not on Wikidata, added by you") and History, with Review changes before saving. Your own edits win over provider facts, the Timeline stays read-only, and a thing you added by hand can be deleted. A media item's Match tab names its Universe with an **Open Universe** link that asks about unsaved changes first; media editors otherwise contain no Universe shortcut or mode switch.
- The Universe explorer (`/universe/{Qid}/explore`) works on phones: the graph is a full-width canvas at most 70% of the viewport height with pinch-zoom, drag-pan and 44 px Zoom in, Zoom out, Fit and Reset buttons, and on every device a label-sorted list of every entity (type and relationship count, one real button per row) sits beside or under it, so a keyboard, remote or screen reader can reach each node and open the same detail panel a graph tap opens.
- Follow server-readable section capabilities; label/filter Organization Members and Event Participants as their own projections without replacing the full Relationships section. Keep the Universe category rail in one row and use keyboard-accessible, anchored, viewport-bounded category/sibling selector popovers.
- Managed EntityAsset artwork is owned by its Universe/entity target. Preserve work-scoped role/context/anchor/time/spoiler qualifiers and provenance. Do not conflate real-world dates, structural order, in-universe narrative time, and editor History.

### Unified player (November 2026)

One shell (`PlaybackShell`) draws the bar for music, audiobooks, TV and movies from `PlaybackControlCatalog` and the playing item. The two persistent hosts (`ListenNowPlayingBar` for audio and `VideoPlaybackHost` for video) keep the media elements (one `<audio>` and one `<video>` for the whole session), the playback wiring and the panels they own, so changing between the bar, the Now playing page, the video overlay and fullscreen never restarts anything. The order, names and defaults of every control are written once in the shared player contract (`clients/contracts/v1/player-controls.md`), generated from the Dashboard's `PlaybackControlCatalog` and checked by a contracts test; the TV and car apps read the same file.

- **Bar.** A three-column row (equal outer columns, so Play is at the true center): artwork (square for music and audiobooks, 16:9 for TV and movies) and title on the left, the transport in the middle, the tools on the right. Music is Shuffle, Previous track, Play, Next track, Repeat, with no skip-by-seconds buttons; Previous restarts a song after `music_previous_restart_seconds` (3) of play, and within the first 3 seconds, or on a second press within 3 seconds of a restart, goes to the previous song. Audiobooks, TV and movies are Previous, Skip back 10 s, Play, Skip forward 30 s, Next. A control that cannot act is dimmed in place; a tool the media or app does not support is absent.
- **Tools.** Music: Lyrics, Queue, Bookmarks, Volume, Now playing, Close. Audiobooks: Chapters, Playback speed (the value, "1.25×"), Sleep timer, Bookmarks, Volume, Now playing, Close. TV: Episodes, Audio & subtitles, Bookmarks, Volume, Playback settings, Fullscreen, Close. Movies: the same without Episodes. Folder videos and courses: Lessons in place of Episodes. Now playing is an icon button, selected while the page is open. Close is the only action that stops and dismisses the player.
- **Panels.** Every pop-up is one `PlaybackPanel`: a menu (272px, 44px rows) or a panel (360 to 420px; Audio & subtitles is 560px and two columns), drawn in the top layer so nothing clips it (inside the fullscreen element in fullscreen), anchored to its trigger without an arrow, with a fixed header (title, back, close) and footer and only the middle scrolling. One top-level panel is open at a time. Escape, the close button, the tool's own button or an outside click closes it and returns focus to the trigger. Closing a panel never changes playback.
- **Queue.** Up next and History tabs; upcoming songs reorder by drag, keyboard or the row menu's Move up and Move down through the guarded `reorder-upcoming` command, using the expected queue revision and saved occurrence IDs. The current item never moves, and Clear removes only the upcoming items.
- **Now playing page.** `/listen/now-playing` for music and audiobooks, with Back to library and Close at the top (both return to the previous page without stopping playback), a large cover, the facts, and tabs (music: Lyrics, Queue, History; audiobooks: Chapters, About). The bar stays at the bottom and the page shares its audio element. There is no pop-out window.
- **Phone.** At 720px wide or narrower, or on a phone form factor (decided once by `PlaybackFormFactor`), the bar is a 56px collapsed strip that reserves its height at the bottom of every page: Play and Next for music, Play and Skip forward 30 s for audiobooks, Play alone for video, with no Favorite. Tapping it expands Now playing, which on a phone is the expanded player itself: a top line (collapse, "Playing from …", "..." menu), the artwork, the title with Favorite (and Like and Dislike for music), the timeline, the transport with a large Play, and one row of at most four tools (music: Shuffle, Lyrics, Queue, Repeat; audiobooks: Chapters or History, Playback speed, Sleep timer, Bookmarks). Bookmarks for music, Playback settings and the item actions are in the "..." menu. There is no volume control. Panels are bottom sheets on a phone and on a touch tablet up to 1024px wide.
- **Play on another device.** A "Play on another device…" row in the "..." Item actions menu (music and audiobooks) and in Playback settings (TV and movies), never on the bar. It opens the browser's own Remote Playback picker (AirPlay in Safari, Google Cast in Chrome and Edge); it is disabled with "Not available in this browser" without the API (Firefox) and with "Not available for Vault items" for a Vault item, which never casts. The Dashboard may start a cast with a signed-in session, for audio and video, through the Engine's short-lived cast link. While casting, the identity zone adds a "Playing on …" line ("another device" when the receiver has no name), the local element is silent and Volume hides; the same row reads "Stop playing on …".
- **Lock screen and system controls.** One metadata builder serves every media: title, the bar's second line, artwork at 512 and 1024px, and the position, duration and speed every second. Music sends Play, Pause, Previous track, Next track and Seek; audiobooks and video send Play, Pause, Skip back, Skip forward and Seek (a TV episode with a next episode adds Next). A hidden episode title is never on the second line, and a Vault item sends nothing while the Vault is locked.
- **Keyboard and motion.** Every control is reachable by Tab with a visible focus ring, touch targets are 44px, and with reduced motion panels and the overlay use only simple fades.
- **Media Session.** Metadata and actions bind to the main audio or video owner.
- **Identity links.** Player identity links use `detailOrigin.fresh`, so the destination opens at the top, including the detail shell's `.context-sidebar-shell__main` scroller. Ordinary Back restoration is preserved.
- **Shared controls.** Dashboard controls use first-party `Components/Shared/App*` primitives, including `AppSelect`/`AppTypedSelect`, `AppTooltip`, `AppRangeSlider`, `AppProgressBar`, and `AppSpinner`. Their native HTML, SVG, CSS and JavaScript own sizing, appearance, focus and expanded/selected semantics. Pages use the shared components rather than reimplementing their controls. A tooltip (`AppTooltip`, `AppInfoTip`, `playback-tooltip.js`) is a manual popover in the browser's top layer and, when a modal dialog is open, sits inside that dialog, so a dialog or its backdrop can never cover it. A section heading carries one (i) (`AppInfoTip`), never one per option; quiet explanations are `AppQuietNote` lines, and amber is only for problems.
- **Intrinsic selectors.** Selectors such as the series selector size to the selected label within the available width, then ellipsize with a full-label tooltip.
- **Follow-up.** Shared controls and Release CSS minification now use first-party ownership. Bundle acceptance still requires measured Release assets and paired visual evidence; broader per-render interop optimization remains separate.
- **Docs toolchain.** The documentation toolchain requires Astro Starlight, pinned in the `tuvima-docs` repository.

## Vault mode and the Vault (Dashboard)

- **Where the Engine says yes, and only there.** The Dashboard asks the Engine whether the signed-in profile can use Vault mode. A profile that is Off, a Kid's profile and any app get "not found", so the profile page, the profile menu, Search, the navigation and Add folder draw nothing about it: no switch, no check, no PIN pad, no word. It is not disabled, it is absent. A Kids profile is not even asked.
- **One PIN.** There is no separate Vault PIN. The profile's own PIN is the only PIN: it protects switching to the profile, and, when the person turns the matching switch on, their admin pages and Vault mode. Wrong tries at any of these doors count together (five wrong tries pause the PIN for fifteen minutes). A profile without a PIN simply has no PIN questions; admin pages and, if the administrator allowed it, Vault mode open without one.
- **The switch and the button.** A person who can use it finds a Vault mode switch on their own profile page (the single place to turn it on), an optional "Show a Vault mode check in the top menu" switch and a choice "Turn Vault mode off after being idle for" (5, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, or Never when no PIN is asked; 60 minutes unless changed). Pressing Vault mode asks for the profile PIN on a number pad when the profile asks for one, otherwise it turns on at once and stays on. Vault mode belongs to the device session: pressing Escape twice, the menu check, switching profile, signing out and closing the app all switch it off and return to Home, and it switches itself off after the idle time while real use of the page keeps it on.
- **The area.** While Vault mode is on a fourth entry, Vault, joins Read, Watch, Listen and View, and Search gets a separate Vault group. The area has a Blur toggle at its top (and at the top of the Vault search results) that starts blurred every time the area unlocks, viewing options (layout, group by, sort, covers, titles) remembered per profile on that device, and the views Recently added, Scenes, Folders, Unmatched, Images, Performers, Studios and Tags. The tab title is plain.
- **Administrators.** The profile page of an adult offers two switches for Vault mode: "Vault mode for X" and "Ask for their PIN to open Vault mode" (on when Vault mode is first turned on for a profile that has a PIN; turning it off first says "Anyone using this profile will be able to open the Vault."). Asking for a PIN needs the profile to have one, and a PIN that either switch depends on cannot be removed until the switch is off. Granting access asks for the administrator's own profile PIN again, and an administrator with no profile PIN is asked to set one first. Separately, each administrator profile has "Ask for my PIN on admin pages" (off by default; needs a PIN). Vault library settings sit inside the area, and the third Add folder choice (Vault) appears only for administrators while Vault mode is on.
- **Vault libraries in Settings > Libraries.** There is no Vault card or tile. The Vault is a fifth area, Vault, beside Watch, Listen, Read and View; a Vault library is created with the Add library inside it, which offers every content type including Adult content (which exists only there), and is never converted to or from an ordinary library. Each content type has at most one ordinary and one Vault library. The area, its libraries, **Move to Vault** are drawn only for a server administrator whose Vault mode is unlocked (never a Kid's profile); for anyone else there is no row, word or hint. **Move to Vault** shows a preview only for now and moves nothing. A Vault library's Lookup tab has a "Look up details online" switch (off by default) that is stored but sends nothing until a later update, and its Activity tab points to the Vault.

## Readers: one frame

The book reader and the page reader (comics and PDFs) sit in the same frame, so a book, a PDF and a comic look and work alike. Only the page in the middle differs.

- **Top line.** The title (with the author, issue or series under it) on the left, then the tools in one order: **Search**, **Contents**, **Pages**, **Bookmark this page**, **Bookmarks**, **Reading settings**, and **Close** as the last control at the top right. A reader leaves out a tool it does not have: a book has no Pages grid, a comic has no Search or Contents, and a PDF has all six. There is no Back arrow; Close returns to where the person came from.
- **Bookmark this page.** One toggle in every reader: an outline bookmark when the page is not bookmarked, a filled bookmark when the person has bookmarked it. Pressing it again removes the person's own bookmark.
- **Panels.** Every tool opens the same panel: 400 px wide on a computer with a dimmed page behind it (a bottom sheet on a phone), the title at the top left and Close at its top right. The **Bookmarks** panel has the **Mine | Everyone** switch (Mine first, and shown first), the **Bookmark this page** button, then the rows. A book lists its highlights beneath the bookmarks on Mine, under the same title, "Bookmarks". The panels' list rows, search box, hints and settings fields are the frame's own, so Search, Contents, Pages and Reading settings read the same in every reader.
- **Phone.** Only **Bookmark this page** and **Close** stay on the top line. Every other tool is in one "..." bottom sheet, in the same order.
- **Background.** The frame reads three colours (the page background, the bar text and the accent). A book's Light, Sepia, Dark and System themes set them, and so do the page reader's Black, Dark and Light backgrounds. The top line and bottom line fade after a few seconds of reading and return on a middle tap, mouse movement to the top or bottom edge, or any key that does not turn a page.
- **Opening and "can't open".** A reader shows the same small spinner while it opens and the same "This file can't be opened" message, with a way back to the library, when it cannot.

## Book reader pictures, highlights, bookmarks and settings

- **Pictures.** Every EPUB opens in this one reader. It opens the book file once and unpacks pictures itself, so illustrations, maps, inline figures, CSS backgrounds and full-page covers appear on every device without separate picture requests. A picture whose label inside the book is missing or wrong is still shown (the reader works out the kind from the file). A picture that is damaged or unsupported shows a small "Picture unavailable" note in its place and the rest of the page is untouched. Pictures from outside the book are never fetched. The old chapter-by-chapter reader and the `?reader=new` switch are gone; reading statistics were not carried over (in-book text search was brought back, see Search below).

- **Tools.** The top line has Search in this book, Contents, Bookmark this page, Bookmarks and Reading settings, then Close at the top right (see Readers: one frame; a book has no Pages grid). Each tool opens one panel: a side panel on tablets and desktops, a bottom sheet on phones (about three quarters of the height at most), closed by the dimmed page, its Close button or Escape.
- **Search.** The magnifier in the top bar (or Ctrl or Cmd + F while the book has focus, since the browser's own Find cannot see inside a book) opens a panel with a search box. Press Enter or the magnifier with two or more letters and the reader looks through every page of the book on the device, ignoring capital letters and accents. Each place found shows the contents entry it sits under and the words around it with the match marked; choosing one closes the panel and goes there, and the matches on the pages shown stay outlined until the next search. The list stops at 50 places and says so; a search with no matches says so plainly. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Highlights.** Selecting text shows a small toolbar under the selection with five colours and "highlight and add a note". Tapping an existing highlight opens a card with its colour, its note, Delete and Done; closing the card saves a changed note. Highlights are saved per profile, anchored by a standard EPUB position (CFI range), so they stay on the same words when the text size or width changes. Highlights made in the old reader are not shown. Devices without text selection (a TV remote) can still see highlights and use bookmarks, contents and settings.
- **Bookmarks.** The bookmark button on the top line is a toggle: filled when this page is bookmarked, and pressing it again removes the bookmark. "Bookmark this page" saves the current place with the section name and percent as its label; a place that is already bookmarked says so instead of adding a duplicate. The Bookmarks panel has the Mine | Everyone switch, lists Mine newest first with the highlights beneath, opens a bookmark and deletes it.
- **Settings.** Theme (light, dark, sepia, auto), text size, line spacing, typeface (the book's own, serif, sans), page width (narrow, standard, wide) and layout (pages or one scroll). They apply immediately. The reader opens with the profile's reading defaults (`/settings/reading`), setting by setting: a setting the person changed in the reader on this device wins over the profile value, and a setting never changed on this device uses the profile value (so a later change to the profile still reaches it). A change in the reader is remembered on the device and also saved to the profile a moment later, and only the setting that changed is written to either. **Reset to my defaults** in the reader's settings panel clears this device's choices, including any saved before this feature, and shows the profile's values again.
- **Keyboard and remote.** Arrow keys, Page Up/Down and Space turn pages while the page has focus; while a toolbar, panel or card has focus, the arrow keys are left to it so a remote can move between controls. Enter or OK on the page shows or hides the bars. Escape closes the card, then the panel, then leaves the book.
- **Phones and TVs.** Bars respect safe areas, panels become bottom sheets under 600px, and controls grow on screens 1600px and wider.
- **Nothing spills past the page.** On reflowable books every element is held to its page column: wide tables and fixed-width blocks shrink to fit, long unbroken words and dotted leaders wrap, and `white-space: nowrap` is set back to normal, so a printed contents list on a front page never leaves a clipped continuation on the next page. Fixed-layout books are left exactly as published. `scripts/visual-qa/book-reader/run-checks.mjs` measures this at 390, 820, 1280 and 1920 px.

## Page reader (comics and PDFs)

- **Which reader.** Read, Continue and Resume on a comic (`.cbz`, `.cbr`, `.cb7`) or a PDF open the page reader at `/read/{AssetId}`; an EPUB opens the book reader at the same address. The Engine says which (`GET /read/{id}/format`), and a file Library cannot read shows "This file can't be opened" with Back to Library. Full screen, no navigation bar.
- **Top line and slider.** The top line shows the title with the issue or author under it, and the tools in the shared order: Search (PDF), Contents (PDF with an outline), Pages, Bookmark this page, Bookmarks and Reading settings, then Close at the top right (there is no Back arrow). The bottom line is the page slider with the page count (*Page 12 of 180*, *Pages 12-13 of 180* for a spread). Both fade after 3 seconds and return on a middle tap, mouse movement to the top or bottom edge, or any key that does not turn a page; they stay while focus is in them. Hovering or dragging the slider shows a small picture of the page under it; with **Show bookmarks on the bar** on, a gold mark sits at each bookmarked page.
- **Turning.** Click or tap the left or right third, press the arrow keys, Page Up/Down or space, or swipe. With a right-to-left direction the sides swap. Under Scrolling, and in Width fit, the page scrolls natively and the keys move it. One more turn past the last page shows the next owned issue of the Series (*Read next* or *Back to the Series*) when there is one; with none, nothing happens. Escape closes the end card, then a panel, then the reader.
- **Settings (kept on the profile).** Layout (Auto, One page, Two pages, Scrolling; remembered per title), Fit (Page, Width), Direction (Left to right, Right to left; comics only, remembered per Series, and set from the comic's own information for manga), Background (Black, Dark, Light), and the Show bookmarks on the bar switch. Auto shows two pages side by side (the cover alone, a double-page spread alone) in a window at least 1180 wide and wider than tall, otherwise one.
- **Pages panel.** A grid of small page pictures that load as they scroll into view; the current page is outlined and a bookmarked page carries a gold bookmark. Choosing a page goes there.
- **PDF.** Search looks through the file's own text on the device (two or more letters, capital letters and accents ignored), lists up to 200 places with the words around them, and choosing one goes to the page. Contents lists the PDF's outline and marks the section being read. A scanned PDF says "This file has no text to search." Links inside a PDF are not clickable.
- **Saved page.** The page is saved a few seconds after the last turn and when the reader closes, as progress with the page and page count; Continue reopens there on any device signed in to the same profile, and a finished title reopens at the start.
- **Bookmarks.** The bookmark button on the top line bookmarks the page on screen (a filled bookmark means this page is bookmarked; pressing again removes it). The Bookmarks panel has the **Mine | Everyone** switch (Mine first); each row shows a small picture of the page, *Page 14*, the label or owner and the date, and choosing it goes there. Delete (own bookmarks only) asks once. A page bookmark is private until an administrator adds it to Everyone's in the editor's Bookmarks tab, which also compares and exports page bookmarks (*Same spot*, *Close* or *Far* by pages apart, from the `page` block of `config/bookmarks.json`). Vault titles keep no page bookmarks in any shared list.
- **TV remote and keyboard.** Left and right turn pages and OK shows the top line and moves into it, where left and right move between the tools, down goes to the slider, up comes back, and Back closes a panel and then the reader. Every tool has an accessible name, the page number is announced to screen readers on each turn, and nothing animates under reduced motion.
- **Phone.** One page, panels as bottom sheets, only Bookmark this page and Close on the top line with the other tools in a "..." sheet, edge taps and swipes turn pages, pinch and double tap zoom; in landscape Auto shows two pages on a tablet and one on a phone.
- **Downloads and offline reading, and the native phone, TV and car apps, are not part of this reader.**

## Wording and language

- Every word shown in the Dashboard comes from the string table (`SharedStrings.resx`) through `IStringLocalizer`, never typed into a component. English (US) is the master copy; `en-GB`, `en-AU` and `en-CA` override only spelling, and `fr`, `de` and `es` are translations. A missing translation falls back to English.
- Product words live once in the glossary (`Glossary_*` keys) and every screen reuses them, so a word such as Favorites has one name everywhere. Text shared by several files uses `Common_*` keys.
- Language and spelling are household settings (**Settings → General → Language and region**); sign-in and first-run follow the server language. Profiles choose only media languages: ordered subtitle languages, lyrics language, audio language and when to show subtitles.
- Use whole sentences with placeholders, never joined fragments, and give plural forms separate keys. Layouts must flex because text can grow 30 to 40 percent in other languages.
- Plugins ship their own `strings/<tag>.json` files, list `supported_languages` in the manifest and follow the server display language, with English as the fallback.
- `TypedWordingGuardrailTests` keeps a count of typed-in wording that may only go down, and fails on missing keys, unused keys and translation placeholder mismatches.

## Home, detail and tiles (November 2026)

This is the current look. Where an older paragraph above says something different, this section wins.

**Bigger, calmer covers.** Every cover is portrait and Watch and Read covers are larger (about six across on a desktop at 1440 px). Nothing changes shape on hover; the cover gets a thick white border with a purple ring. *Why:* tiles that grow or turn landscape push their neighbours around and make the page feel busy; a border says "this one" without moving anything.

**Continue is its own landscape row.** Movies and shows use the backdrop with the title or logo drawn on it (the title is always there, so a tile is never blank). Books and audiobooks show the cover on a soft copy of itself with the title, author and place. A playlist or one album is one tile and "All songs" on shuffle is a collage of album art. Each tile has an X that hides it from Continue; playing the item again brings it back. *Why:* the person is most likely to want to carry on, so that row is the widest and clearest on the page, and the X replaces the old "Hide from Continue" menu entry.

**Shorter hero.** About 55 percent of the screen height. Slides take turns across Watch, Read and Listen, with whatever the person is in the middle of first inside each kind. There is no "Featured content" label and only two buttons: the main action and +. *Why:* a banner dominated by one kind (for example all new books) hid the rest of the library, and extra buttons duplicated the detail page.

**Small square labels.** "New" and "New Episode" are small square tags at the top-left of a cover. Every pill and label in the app is square-cornered (3 px). Round stays for avatars, switches, progress bars, the player dock and seek bubbles. *Why:* one shape for every label makes the app read as one family.

**One short "..." menu.** On the detail page it holds Restart (only if started), Mark as watched (only while the title is not finished), Edit (desktop, if allowed) and Delete (if allowed). Mark as unwatched, viewing history, Hide from Continue and Add to collection are gone from the menu; collections are made on the Collections screen and the full history is under Settings > Activity.

**Detail page order.** Title, facts, the main button, then one row of small icons (+, thumbs, "..."). *Why:* the thing a person came to do sits right under what the title is.

**People pictures.** Small and square-cornered (88 px) everywhere except the person page, which opens with a large portrait (about 340 px on a desktop).

## One tile design (November 2026)

Every cover in Library is the same tile, on every page. This section replaces the earlier tile size, hover and caption rules above.

- **One size.** A poster is 214 × 321 px on a desktop and 112 × 168 px on a phone; a square is 321 × 321 px beside posters and 214 × 214 px on a page that holds only squares (Listen); a landscape tile is 320 × 180 px. There is no tile size control anywhere and no `tile=` address part. A wider window shows more columns, not bigger tiles.
- **No text under a tile.** A cover carries its own title in its art. Only two tiles draw words of their own: the Continue tile (title, then rating and year, under its picture) and a cover with no art (a plain dark cover with an icon, the title and the author or artist; a video gets a still on top and its name and one detail line below, at poster size). Collection, Series, Universe and album tiles carry their name on the art; People tiles keep their name line. Episode lists on a show or season page keep the episode number and title under each still.
- **One "..." button.** It is always visible at the top right (a 44 × 44 px tap area, a vertical three-dot mark with no box). It holds Add to My List, Restart (once started), Open Universe, Edit (desktop) and Delete (administrators on a desktop), in that order; there is no "+" button. A Continue tile's menu starts with Go to (the show, movie or book) and holds Remove from Continue after Restart.
- **One hover.** A 2 px purple outline around the cover only. The tile never grows, lifts or opens a panel. A touch device gets the same tile with no hover and no text.
- **Two flush labels.** A status label touches the top-left corner ("Getting details" while the item is being matched, otherwise New or New episode, which the Engine decides) and a bottom label touches the bottom-left corner (the count of a group: "12 episodes", "6 books", "3 videos").
- **Home rolls up per parent.** Recently Added shows one tile per show, album, Series of books, audiobooks or comics, or folder of videos, with its count in the bottom label; movies, photos and loose files stay one tile each. Opening a rolled-up tile goes to that parent's own page.
