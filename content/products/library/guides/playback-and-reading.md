# Play, listen, and read

Start an item in Tuvima Library in a few steps. Use its reader or player to control your session. Your active [profile](../reference/glossary.md#profile) keeps its own progress and settings.

## Read a book or comic

1. Open **Read** and select a book or comic.
2. Choose **Read** or the resume action on its detail page.
3. Use the reader's page controls and other tools.
4. Return to the library when you finish the session.

The EPUB reader opens supported ebooks in the browser. Reading tools and progress depend on the format. Use playback and reading settings to change the options offered.

## Play music

1. Open **Listen > Music**.
2. Select a song, or open an album and choose **Play** or **Shuffle**.
3. Use the bottom audio dock to play, pause, or seek.
4. Expand the player for more room and tools.

**Queue** shows upcoming songs. **History** shows past playback. **Lyrics** shows text when available and has a close button on a computer. It highlights words or lines when the source has valid timing. Untimed lyrics stay readable text. A small line under the lyrics says where they came from (LRCLIB, or a file next to the song). When a song has none, the panel explains that lyrics come from a .lrc file beside the song or from LRCLIB, and anyone can choose **Look up lyrics now** or **Add a lyrics file**. In **Up Next**, drag a song by the handle on its left; **History** shows when each song was played. Moving a song announces itself to screen readers only; a failed move shows a short notice.

## Listen to an audiobook

1. Open a book in **Listen > Audiobooks**.
2. Choose start or resume, or select a track on Overview.
3. Open **Chapters** to browse the source segments and jump to one. The bar shows the chapter you are in (for example "Chapter 4 of 21"), with the time in the chapter and the time left in the book; the **Chapter | Book** switch at the end of the bar flips it to the whole book. Your choice is remembered on this device. The bar never draws chapter ticks; ticks are only for your bookmarks.
4. Use bookmarks and History to return to saved or past positions.
5. Change **Speed** or set **Sleep** when needed.

Audiobooks keep track progress and offer bookmarks, speed, history, and sleep tools. Videos have the same Bookmarks panel (see below). Chapter titles come from the source unless you edit them. Chapter times and boundaries stay file facts.

Sleep offers timed choices and chapter ends that the player can verify. A chapter option may be disabled if its end cannot be checked.

On desktop, **Close player** saves a checked, paused position and ends the audio session. On a phone, collapsing the player makes it smaller and keeps audio playing.

## Watch a movie or TV episode

1. Open **Watch** and select a movie or TV show.
2. Choose **Watch** or **Resume**. A show starts an in-progress episode or the first owned episode.
3. Use the bar at the bottom. The title is on the left (for example "From E4 · Episode name"); Play sits in the middle with back and forward 10 seconds, and previous and next episode when there are some; the speaker button and the tools you need for this title are on the right, then Full screen. Every control is large, and a control grows a little and shows its name when you point at it or tab to it. The speaker button opens a dark volume box with an upright slider when you point at it; click anywhere on the track to set the level, or click the button to mute. The speaker shows sound on until you mute it or turn the level to zero. When you pause, the picture dims and the details appear on the left: the show, season and episode with its description, or a film's title, year and length. A rated title also shows its rating at the top left, on pause and for the first few seconds of playback. A title with no rating shows no rating.
4. Choose **Close** to return to the movie or the episode's show details.

Each kind of video shows only the tools that fit it:

| You are watching | Tools on the right |
| --- | --- |
| A movie | Bookmarks, Settings, Speed |
| A TV episode | Episodes (the season list, with what plays next), Bookmarks, Settings, Speed, and Next episode |
| Other video that belongs to a series | Lessons, Bookmarks, Settings, Speed |

Episode lists and what plays next use episodes you own. A provider listing alone is not playable. Audio tracks, subtitles and quality depend on the file and server. **Settings** (the gear) has **Audio**, **Subtitles** and **Styling**. Audio and Subtitles list every language in the file; if your browser cannot switch audio on a file it plays directly, Library asks the Engine for the same video with every audio track and carries on from the same second after a short "Switching audio…". If you have not switched on a subtitle source, Subtitles says so and the "find subtitles" row is hidden. **Styling** sets subtitle size, font, shadow, colour and background; the change shows at once and is saved on your profile.

Video can play directly when the client supports the source. Other files may need browser-ready delivery first. That can delay playback.

## Bookmarks in books

In the book reader, open **Bookmarks and highlights** and choose **Bookmark this page**. A bookmark marks the page you are on. A small **Global** tag marks a bookmark shared with everyone who can see the book. Which bookmarks everyone sees is chosen by an administrator in the editor (see [Bookmarks for everyone](#bookmarks-for-everyone)). Bookmarks you made before this arrived are still yours.

## Bookmarks in videos and audiobooks

In the video player, choose **Bookmarks**. The pane opens above the bar, tall enough to browse a long list. **Add bookmark here** (it shows the time) saves a personal bookmark in one click; type a note first if you like, and press Enter to save it with the note. There is no keyboard shortcut for bookmarks, so a stray key press never adds one.

Under the add row the pane lists the title's bookmarks in time order. When the title has both kinds, a filter appears: **All**, **Mine** or **Global**. Library remembers your choice on your profile. **Mine** are yours alone. **Global** bookmarks are shared with everyone who can see the title; they are read-only in the player, and an administrator changes them in the editor. Each row has **Play** and, for ones you may change, **Delete** (it asks "Delete?" in place).

By default the progress bar stays clean. Switch on **Show bookmarks on the bar** in the pane (or in **Settings > Playback**) to see a small round bubble for each bookmark: the accent colour for yours, white for Global. Point at a bubble (or tap it on a touch screen) to see its name, time and note, with Play; if it has a longer description, choose **More**. Chapter ticks are off for video by default and on for audiobooks; both are in **Settings > Playback**.

The audiobook player keeps its panel with **Add** and **Saved**. Add captures the moment the panel opens, so it is not lost while you type. It shows the chapter name when there is one, lets you preview the captured spot, and takes a short note (up to 200 characters). Saved lists the title's bookmarks, newest first, with jump-to and delete. Global bookmarks show "· Global". The audiobook progress bar draws chapter ticks but no bookmark bubbles, so there is nothing to switch off there.

Adding a bookmark works on a phone or tablet and does not need Editing Mode.

## Bookmarks for everyone

An administrator decides which bookmarks everyone sees in the editor, on a computer: open a movie, episode, video, audiobook or book, choose **Edit** in the "..." menu and open the **Bookmarks** tab. The upper list is **Everyone's bookmarks**, exactly as the player shows them. Until you change anything it is pooled from every profile's own bookmarks, with bookmarks at the same spot merged. Your first change (**Rename**, **Change time** or **Remove from Everyone's**) turns it into the title's **definitive set**, and from then on the player shows only that set. **Start over from profiles**, in the list's "..." menu, discards the set and pools again. The lower list, **From profiles**, shows every profile's own bookmarks; **Add to Everyone's** puts one in the set and its owner keeps it as their own. On a video each row starts with the frame at that moment.

**Export** saves a title's Everyone's bookmarks as one bookmark file. A pooled list exports only times and names, never anyone's notes; a definitive set keeps its names and notes. **Import** reads a bookmark file and lines each bookmark up beside the nearest one in Everyone's, as **Same spot**, **Close**, **Far** or **New**, with the sensible choice already ticked. A book uses places in the text instead of times, so it has Export and Import but no Change time. A show, season or album has an overview instead: one row per episode or track with Everyone's count, whether it is a definitive set or pooled, and the count from profiles, plus **Export the show** and **Import for the show**, which go episode by episode. Like every edit, nothing is saved until **Save changes**, and bookmarks at the same spot are merged then. The tab is not offered in the Vault or to a Kids profile.

## Resume and save separately

Continue shelves and resume actions follow progress. Completed items show their completion state. Albums, tracks, and View items do not use long-form completion bars.

**My List** saves an item for later. **Rate** means Like or Dislike. **Favorite** is a separate choice. A song's heart adds it to Favorites. These choices do not start playback or replace progress.

## Now Playing

While something is playing, a small animated sound-bars icon sits in the top bar. For music and audiobooks it opens the Now Playing page over the page you are on, and clicking it again (or Collapse) brings you back; clicking the cover art or title in the bottom bar does the same. For video it returns you to the player. Playback never stops when you leave the page. Starting an audiobook from its details page opens the player above that page, so it can never sit hidden underneath.

## Keyboard

Space plays and pauses, the arrow keys skip and change volume, and Escape closes the player or a panel. These stay because they are basic accessibility, and Library never shows them as hints on screen. There are no bookmark shortcuts, so a stray key press can never add one.
