# Play, listen, and read

Start an item in Tuvima Library in a few steps. Use its reader or player to control your session. Your active [profile](../reference/glossary.md#profile) keeps its own progress and settings.

Music, audiobooks, TV episodes and movies all use the same player: a bar at the bottom of the page with the title on the left, the playback buttons in the middle and the tools on the right. Only the tools change with what you are playing, so once you know one you know them all. The books and comics reader is separate and is described first.

## Read a book or comic

1. Open **Read** and select a book or comic.
2. Choose **Read** or the resume action on its detail page.
3. Use the reader's page controls and other tools.
4. Return to the library when you finish the session.

The EPUB reader opens supported ebooks in the browser. Books, PDFs and comics share one reader frame: the same top line, the same tool order (Search, Contents, Pages, Bookmark this page, Bookmarks, Reading settings), the same side panels and **Close** at the top right. A reader leaves out a tool it does not have. On a phone only Bookmark this page and Close stay on the top line, and the other tools are in a "..." sheet. Use playback and reading settings to change the options offered.

Comics (CBZ, CBR, CB7) and PDFs open in the page reader. It keeps your place, shows the title under Continue, and offers the same Bookmarks as books. Turn pages with the arrow keys, Page Up and Down, the space bar, a click or tap on the left or right third of the page, or a swipe; Ctrl and the wheel, a pinch or a double tap zoom. The top line (title, Search and Contents for a PDF, Pages, Bookmark this page, Bookmarks, Reading settings, then Close) and the page slider fade after three seconds and return on a middle tap or any key that does not turn a page. Layout (Auto, One page, Two pages, Scrolling), Fit, Direction (comics) and Background are remembered on your profile, so they follow you to another device. On a TV remote, left and right turn pages, OK shows the top line, and Back closes a panel and then the reader.

## How the player works

### The bar

When you start something, a bar appears at the bottom of every page. It has a thin timeline across the top, then one row in three parts:

- **Left:** the artwork (movies and TV episodes show theirs too) and the title with a second line, such as artist and album, author and chapter, or show and "S1 E1". Names are links to their pages. Music adds **Favorite**, **Like**, **Dislike** and a "..." menu beside the song. Audiobooks, TV and movies add Favorite where it applies, and the "..." menu.
- **Middle:** the playback buttons, with **Play** exactly in the center. They do not move, whatever the title or the tools.
- **Right:** the tools for this kind of media, in a fixed order. A tool you cannot use right now is dimmed where it is, and a tool this title does not support is simply not there, so nothing is a dead button.

Every control is a large button with a soft highlight under the pointer or keyboard focus. Point at one, or tab to it, to see its name.

| You are playing | Playback buttons, left to right | Tools on the right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Music | Shuffle, Previous track, Play, Next track, Repeat | Lyrics, Queue, Bookmarks, Volume, Now playing, Close |
| An audiobook | Previous chapter, Skip back 10 s, Play, Skip forward 30 s, Next chapter | Chapters, Speed (shows the current speed, for example "1.25×"), Sleep timer, Bookmarks, Volume, Now playing, Close |
| A TV episode | Previous episode, Skip back 10 s, Play, Skip forward 30 s, Next episode | Episodes, Audio & subtitles, Bookmarks, Volume, Playback settings, Fullscreen, Close |
| A movie | Previous, Skip back 10 s, Play, Skip forward 30 s, Next | Audio & subtitles, Bookmarks, Volume, Playback settings, Fullscreen, Close |
| A folder video or lesson | Previous, Skip back 10 s, Play, Skip forward 30 s, Next | Lessons (when other videos follow), Audio & subtitles, Bookmarks, Volume, Playback settings, Fullscreen, Close |

Previous and Next mean a song, a chapter or an episode. A movie with nothing to go to shows both dimmed.

Music skips whole songs, not seconds, so it has no skip buttons. **Previous** restarts the song after it has played for 3 seconds; press it in the first 3 seconds, or twice in a row, to go to the previous song. At the start of the queue it restarts the song.

Skip back is 10 seconds and skip forward is 30 seconds everywhere. Change them in **Playback settings** (skip back 5, 10, 15 or 30 for video and 10, 15 or 30 for audio; skip forward 10, 30, 60 or 90 for video and 15, 30 or 60 for audio); the audio choices are also in **Settings > Listening**. Library saves them on your profile.

### Panels

Every tool opens the same kind of panel, in the same dark style, above the bar (or inside the video, including full screen). Only one panel is open at a time: opening another closes the first. A panel has a fixed title row with a close button, a scrolling middle and, when it has actions, a fixed footer. Press **Escape**, the close button, the tool's own button or anywhere outside the panel to close it; focus goes back to the button. Closing a panel never changes playback.

### Volume

Click the speaker to mute, and click again to bring back the level you had. Point at it, or tab to it, and a short upright slider opens just above it, so nothing else on the bar moves. On a tablet or other touch screen the speaker opens a small volume panel with mute inside it. A phone has no volume control in the player because its own volume buttons do the job.

### Close

**Close** is the only button that stops the player and removes the bar. On a computer it saves your position as paused first. Everything else, including opening Now playing, switching to full screen or collapsing on a phone, leaves playback exactly as it was.

## Play music

1. Open **Listen > Music**.
2. Select a song, or open an album and choose **Play** or **Shuffle**.
3. Use the bar at the bottom to play, pause, skip or seek.
4. Choose **Now playing** to open the full page (see below).

**Lyrics** follow the song and highlight the current line when the source has valid timing. Scroll by hand to read ahead and the highlight stops following; choose **Resume following** to catch up, or select a timed line to jump to it. A "Synced" or "Unsynced" tag says which kind you have, and untimed lyrics are plain text. The footer names where the lyrics came from (LRCLIB, or a file next to the song) and has a "..." menu with **Find another**, **Add a lyrics file** and **About lyrics sources**. When a song has none, the panel offers **Find lyrics** and **Add a lyrics file**, and anyone can use them.

**Queue** has two tabs. **Up next** lists the songs after this one; drag a song by the handle on its left, or use **Move up** and **Move down** in its "..." menu. **Clear** removes the upcoming songs and never stops the one playing. **History** lists what this session has already played and when. The footer says where the queue is playing from, such as an album.

The "..." menu beside the song has **Add to playlist…**, **Mark as listened** (or unlistened), **Listening history**, **Go to album**, **Go to artist** and **Playback settings**.

Music has no sleep timer yet.

## Listen to an audiobook

1. Open a book in **Listen > Audiobooks**.
2. Choose start or resume, or select a track on Overview.
3. Open **Chapters** to browse the book's chapters and jump to one. The panel shows "Chapter 8 of 24" and "Book · 32% complete", a check on chapters you finished, and "12:08 elapsed · 18:32 remaining" on the current one.
4. Change the speed or set a sleep timer when needed.

The timeline says what it shows: "Chapter 8 · 12:08 · −18:32" or "Book · 32% · 6h 12m left". Switch between **Chapter** and **Whole book** at the bottom of the Chapters panel; Library remembers your choice on your profile. The bar never draws chapter ticks unless you ask for them in **Settings > Playback**. Chapter titles come from the source unless you edit them. Chapter times and boundaries stay file facts.

**Speed.** Select the speed on the bar (for example "1.25×") to open the speed panel. It shows the current speed, presets of 0.75×, 1×, 1.25×, 1.5× and 2×, and a slider from 0.5× to 3× in steps of 0.05. A preset is ticked only when the speed matches it exactly, and **Reset to 1×** is in the footer.

**Sleep timer.** Choose **15 min**, **30 min**, **45 min** or **60 min**, **End of chapter** or **Custom duration…**. While it runs the panel says "Stopping in 14:32" and "Playback will pause automatically." An end-of-chapter timer says "Stopping at the end of chapter 8". **+5 minutes** adds five minutes to what is left, and **Cancel timer** ends it. End of chapter is offered when the book has chapters whose end the player can verify, and you can hide it under **Settings > Listening**. The timer belongs to the listening session, so Now playing, the bar and a page reload all show the same countdown.

The "..." menu has **Add to playlist…**, **Mark as finished** (or unfinished), **Listening history**, **Go to book**, **Go to author** and **Playback settings**.

## Now playing

**Now playing** is the full page for music and audiobooks.

- **On a computer or tablet,** choose the **Now playing** tool, click the cover art or title in the bar, or choose the sound-bars icon that appears in the top bar while audio is playing. The page (`/listen/now-playing`) shows a large cover and the facts on the left. For music the tabs are **Lyrics**, **Queue** and **History**. For an audiobook they are **Chapters** and **About** (the description and book facts). The same bar stays at the bottom. **Back to library** and **Close** at the top both return you to the page you came from and never stop playback.
- **On a phone,** tap the collapsed bar and it grows into Now playing (see below).

There is no separate player window to pop out. Starting an audiobook from its details page shows the player above that page, so it can never sit hidden underneath.

## Watch a movie or TV episode

1. Open **Watch** and select a movie or TV show.
2. Choose **Watch** or **Resume**. A show starts an in-progress episode or the first owned episode.
3. Use the bar at the bottom. It appears over the picture and fades after a few seconds of no input while the video plays. It stays while you point at it, while a control has keyboard focus and while a panel is open.
4. Choose **Close** to return to the movie or the episode's show details.

Drag the timeline to scrub. On a video Library has made previews for, a small picture of the target moment appears above the handle; otherwise you see the time only.

**Pause** shows what you are watching at the bottom left, above the bar, on a soft gradient (the picture is not dimmed): the show, the course or the film's title; a line such as "S1 E1 · The First Wish · 22 min" or "2019 · 1h 52m"; and a short description for episodes and lessons. The details hide while a panel is open. A title that has a content rating shows a small card at the top left, for example "Rated TV-MA", on pause and for the first few seconds of playback. It shows the content rating only, never an audience score, and a title with no rating shows no card.

**Audio & subtitles** opens one panel with Audio on the left and Subtitles on the right; a check marks your choice. Audio rows give the language and the file's facts, such as "Dolby Atmos · 7.1", with commentary and audio-description tracks last. Subtitles start with **Off**, then list each track with its label kept, such as "English · SDH" or "English · Forced". A long name stays on one line and ends in "…"; point at it to read it all. A track your browser cannot switch is listed as "Not switchable in this browser". If your browser cannot switch audio on a file it plays directly, Library asks the Engine for the same video with every audio track and carries on from the same second after a short "Switching audio…". Changing either keeps your place within a second. The footer has **Subtitle appearance** and **Find subtitles…** (hidden until a subtitle source is switched on).

**Find subtitles** searches your subtitle source and lists what it found, so you choose rather than hope. It searches in your profile's subtitle language (change it with **Subtitle language** at the top), and each result shows its name, whether it is for the hearing impaired, and **Same release** or **Different release** as a hint about timing. **Use this** downloads that one and shows it at once; your choice applies to everyone watching the title. **This one is wrong** removes a subtitle that does not fit. When nothing comes back, the panel says why in plain words, for example that no subtitles exist in that language, that the title has not been matched yet, that the source did not accept its key, or that it could not be reached, with **Try again** where that helps. Opening Audio & subtitles never searches online by itself; a search only runs when you open Find subtitles.

**Subtitle appearance** has a live preview over a still from the video and three styles of the same size: **Default**, **Large text** and **High contrast**. The background (**None**, **Shadow**, **Box**) is a separate choice, so picking one never changes the other. Open **Custom appearance** for the exact text size, font, colors and opacity. Changes apply at once and are saved on your profile. **Reset to default** is in the footer.

**Playback settings** (the gear) has **Playback speed**, **Skip back**, **Skip forward**, **Picture-in-picture** (when your browser allows it), **Playback information** and **Reset playback preferences**.

**Playback information** answers "what am I actually watching?" in five short sections: **File** (the file's name, type and size), **Video** (format, resolution, frame rate, color such as HDR10 or Dolby Vision, and bitrate), **Audio** (the track playing, its format and channels), **Subtitles** (the one shown and where it came from) and **Delivery** (played as it is or converted, and why, plus the connection). A fact the file does not carry is left out instead of shown blank. **Copy details** puts the whole list on your clipboard, which helps when you ask for help. It shows the file's name only, never the folder it lives in.

**Episodes** lists a show's episodes with a **Season** dropdown, the count ("20 episodes"), and each episode's still, number, title, length and a one-line synopsis. The current one reads "Playing · 2:23 of 22:41" (or "Paused") and the next has an **Up next** tag. Select an episode to play it, or resume it where you left it. The **Autoplay next episode** switch is at the bottom of this panel. Folder videos and courses call the same panel **Lessons**, and never say Episode or Season.

When an episode or lesson ends, a card shows above the bar: **Up next**, the next item with "Next episode" or "Next lesson", and **Cancel** and **Play now**. With autoplay on it counts down ("Starts in 10 seconds"); with autoplay off it waits for you. For a course, a **Homework for this lesson** block lists the lesson's own files and the files shown alongside it, each with **Open**; Open stops the countdown and opens the file, and the player shrinks to its bar. A TV episode's card has no homework. On the last lesson the card shows only when there is homework.

Episode lists and what plays next use episodes you own. A provider listing alone is not playable. Audio tracks, subtitles and quality depend on the file and server.

Video can play directly when the client supports the source. Other files may need browser-ready delivery first. That can delay playback.

## On a tablet or phone

A tablet keeps the same bar with larger touch targets, and its panels open as sheets from the bottom (on touch screens up to 1024 px wide). Nothing depends on hovering: to see the name of an icon, press and hold it.

On a phone, the bar is a small collapsed strip, 56 px tall, at the bottom of every page. It never covers your content. It has the artwork, the title and second line, and only these buttons:

| Playing | Buttons on the collapsed bar |
| --- | --- |
| Music | Play, Next track |
| An audiobook | Play, Skip forward 30 s |
| Video | Play |

Tap the bar to expand it into Now playing for music and audiobooks. At the top are a collapse arrow, "Playing from …" and the "..." menu. Below are the large cover, the title with Favorite (and Like and Dislike for music), the timeline, the playback buttons with a large Play, and one short row of at most four tools:

| Playing | Tool row |
| --- | --- |
| Music | Shuffle, Lyrics, Queue, Repeat |
| An audiobook | Chapters, Speed, Sleep timer, Bookmarks |

An audiobook without chapters has no Chapters tool; nothing takes its place. Everything else, including a song's Bookmarks, Playback settings and the item actions, is in the "..." menu. Collapsing keeps audio playing.

Video on a phone keeps its picture with the same playback buttons and its own row of tools: Audio & subtitles, Episodes (or Lessons), Bookmarks and Fullscreen. Panels are bottom sheets.

## Play on another device

You can send what is playing to a TV or speaker that your browser can see. The row is **Play on another device…**:

- for music and audiobooks, in the "..." menu beside the title;
- for TV and movies, in **Playback settings**.

It is never a button on the bar. It opens your browser's own device picker, so Safari lists AirPlay devices and Chrome and Edge list Google Cast devices. Library does not scan your network or load any outside script; your browser already knows what is nearby.

While it plays elsewhere, the bar adds a line such as "Playing on Living room TV" (or "Playing on another device"), this device goes quiet, and **Volume** is hidden because the TV's remote owns volume. Play, seek, Previous, Next and your tools keep working from the bar. To bring it back, choose **Stop playing on …** in the same place, or close the bar.

The row is dimmed where it cannot work, and says why: "Not available in this browser" (Firefox and other browsers without the picker) or "Not available for Vault items". A Vault item never plays on another device.

To start it, the Dashboard asks the Engine for a short-lived secret link for that one title, which the TV uses to fetch the stream without signing in. The link works for at most 6 hours, for a signed-in session, for video and for audio. It is not limited to your home network: it works wherever your server can be reached, so treat it like a password and do not share it. Signing out, removing the profile, or lowering a Kids limit so the title is no longer allowed stops it. See [Security](../architecture/security.md) for how it is checked.

## Bookmarks in books

In a book, PDF or comic, press the bookmark button on the top line, or open **Bookmarks** and choose **Bookmark this page**. A bookmark marks the page you are on; the button is filled when this page is bookmarked, and pressing it again removes your bookmark. The Bookmarks panel switches between **Mine** and **Everyone**, and a book lists its highlights beneath your bookmarks. A small **Global** tag marks a bookmark shared with everyone who can see the book. Which bookmarks everyone sees is chosen by an administrator in the editor (see [Bookmarks for everyone](#bookmarks-for-everyone)). Bookmarks you made before this arrived are still yours.

## Bookmarks in the player

Choose **Bookmarks** in the player: on the bar for audiobooks and video, and on a phone in the audiobook or video tool row. Music has no bookmarks. The panel opens above the bar.

- **Save a spot.** The wide **+ Bookmark at 34:12** button saves a bookmark at the moment you pressed it, at once, with no note. To add a note, type in **Add a note…** first. There is no keyboard shortcut for bookmarks, so a stray key press never adds one.
- **Mine or Global.** The panel opens on **Mine**, your own bookmarks, and shows nothing of anyone else's. You can **Edit** (rename and add a note) and **Delete** (it asks again in place) your own. If something fails, the panel says so in place ("Couldn't save" or "Couldn't delete"). On movies and TV only, once an administrator has made a Global set for the title, a **Mine | Global** choice appears. **Global** is the set the administrator chose for everyone, each row with its maker's name, and you see it only when you pick it. Global rows have no actions in the player. Books and audiobooks have no Global set, and music has no bookmarks at all.
- **Rows.** Each row shows the time, the name or note, and a picture: a frame from that moment for a video, the cover for audio. Select a row to jump there. A search box appears above eight rows. Empty lists say "No Global bookmarks on this title yet" (Global) or "No bookmarks of yours yet" (Mine).
- **On the timeline.** Your own bookmarks are small dots on the progress bar; if you switch the panel to Global, the dots follow that list. Point at a dot to see its name, time and picture, and click it to jump there; a bookmark you have just added pulses once so you can see where it landed. The **Show on timeline** switch at the bottom of the panel turns the dots off if you prefer a clean bar. Chapter ticks are a separate setting in **Settings > Playback**, off for video and on for audiobooks.

Who sees what is decided by the Engine, not the screen: you see a title's bookmarks if you may see the title. A Vault title has no Global set, and a Kids profile sees Global only for titles it may watch. The player only ever makes your own bookmarks: it has no copy, share, import or "add to Global" action; those belong to the editor.

Adding a bookmark works on a phone or tablet.

## Bookmarks for everyone

An administrator decides which bookmarks everyone sees in the editor, on a computer: open a movie, episode or video, choose **Edit** in the "..." menu and open the **Bookmarks** tab. Books, audiobooks and music have no Global set, so they have no Bookmarks tab. The upper list is **Global**, exactly as the player shows it when someone picks Global. It starts empty: nothing from the profiles is added on its own. **Add to Global** puts a profile's bookmark in the set (its owner keeps it as their own), and **Rename**, **Change time** and **Remove from Global** edit the set. **Clear Global**, in the list's "..." menu, empties it again and leaves everyone's own bookmarks alone. The lower list, **From profiles**, shows every profile's own bookmarks. On a video each row starts with the frame at that moment.

**Export** saves a title's Global bookmarks as one bookmark file. **Import** reads a bookmark file and lines each bookmark up beside the nearest one in Global, as **Same spot**, **Close**, **Far** or **New**, with the sensible choice already ticked. What you import lands in Global. A show, season or album has an overview instead: one row per episode with Global's count, whether a set has been made, and the count from profiles, plus **Export the show** and **Import for the show**, which go episode by episode. Like every edit, nothing is saved until **Save changes**, and bookmarks at the same spot are merged then. The tab is not offered in the Vault or to a Kids profile.

## Lock screen and system controls

Your phone, computer or headset draws its own lock screen or media card; Library fills it in. It shows the same artwork, title and second line as the bar, with the buttons that fit: Previous, Play and Next for music, and back 10 and forward 30 seconds for audiobooks and video, plus a position bar you can drag. A TV episode with a next episode also offers Next. If you hide an episode's title with the Spoilers setting, it is not on the card. A Vault item sends nothing to the lock screen while the Vault is locked.

## Resume and save separately

Continue shelves and resume actions follow progress. Completed items show their completion state. Albums, tracks, and View items do not use long-form completion bars.

**My List** saves an item for later: on a movie, show, book or audiobook the plus adds it to My List and becomes a tick, in the players too. **Favorite** is the heart, kept for songs: a song's heart adds it to Favorites. **Rate** means Like or Dislike, a separate choice that saving never changes. These choices do not start playback or replace progress.

## Keyboard

Every control is reachable with Tab and shows a clear focus ring. Space plays and pauses, the arrow keys step the timeline and the volume, and Escape closes a panel or the player. These stay because they are basic accessibility, and Library never shows them as hints on screen. There are no bookmark shortcuts, so a stray key press can never add one. If your device asks for reduced motion, panels and the video controls use only simple fades and lyrics jump to the current line instead of scrolling smoothly.
