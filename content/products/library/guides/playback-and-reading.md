# Play, listen, and read

Start an item in Tuvima Library in a few steps. Use its reader or player to control your session. Your active [profile](../reference/glossary.md#profile) keeps its own progress and settings.

## Read a book or comic

1. Open **Read** and select a book or comic.
2. Choose **Read** or the resume action on its detail page.
3. Use the reader's page controls and other tools.
4. Return to the library when you finish the session.

The EPUB reader opens supported ebooks in the browser. Reading tools and progress depend on the format. Use playback and reading settings to change the options offered.

## Play music

1. Open **Listen > Music**.
2. Select a song, or open an album and choose **Play** or **Shuffle**.
3. Use the bottom audio dock to play, pause, or seek.
4. Expand the player for more room and tools.

**Queue** shows upcoming songs. **History** shows past playback. **Lyrics** shows text when available and has a close button on a computer. It highlights words or lines when the source has valid timing. Untimed lyrics stay readable text. A small line under the lyrics says where they came from (LRCLIB, or a file next to the song). When a song has none, the panel explains that lyrics come from a .lrc file beside the song or from LRCLIB, and anyone can choose **Look up lyrics now** or **Add a lyrics file**. In **Up Next**, drag a song by the handle on its left; **History** shows when each song was played. Moving a song announces itself to screen readers only; a failed move shows a short notice.

## Listen to an audiobook

1. Open a book in **Listen > Audiobooks**.
2. Choose start or resume, or select a track on Overview.
3. Open **Chapters** to browse the source segments.
4. Use bookmarks and History to return to saved or past positions.
5. Change **Speed** or set **Sleep** when needed.

Audiobooks keep track progress and offer bookmarks, speed, history, and sleep tools. Videos have the same Bookmarks panel (see below). Chapter titles come from the source unless you edit them. Chapter times and boundaries stay file facts.

Sleep offers timed choices and chapter ends that the player can verify. A chapter option may be disabled if its end cannot be checked.

On desktop, **Close player** saves a checked, paused position and ends the audio session. On a phone, collapsing the player makes it smaller and keeps audio playing.

## Watch a movie or TV episode

1. Open **Watch** and select a movie or TV show.
2. Choose **Watch** or **Resume**. A show starts an in-progress episode or the first owned episode.
3. Use the bar at the bottom. Play, back and forward 10 seconds and a speaker button are on the left; the episode name is in the middle (for example "From E4 · Episode name"); the tools you need for this title are on the right, then Full screen. Every control is large, and a control grows a little and shows its name when you point at it or tab to it. The speaker button opens a short upright volume slider when you point at it; click the button to mute. When you pause, the picture dims a little and the show, episode and name appear in the middle until you play again.
4. Choose **Close** to return to the movie or the episode's show details.

Each kind of video shows only the tools that fit it:

| You are watching | Tools on the right |
| --- | --- |
| A movie | Bookmarks, Settings, Speed |
| A TV episode | Episodes (the season list, with what plays next), Bookmarks, Settings, Speed, and Next episode |
| Other video that belongs to a series | Lessons, Bookmarks, Settings, Speed |

Episode lists and what plays next use episodes you own. A provider listing alone is not playable. Audio tracks, subtitles and quality depend on the file and server. **Settings** (the gear) has **Audio**, **Subtitles** and **Styling**. Audio and Subtitles list every language in the file; if your browser cannot switch audio on a file it plays directly, Library asks the Engine for the same video with every audio track and carries on from the same second after a short "Switching audio…". If you have not switched on a subtitle source, Subtitles says so and the "find subtitles" row is hidden. **Styling** sets subtitle size, font, shadow, colour and background; the change shows at once and is saved on your profile.

Video can play directly when the client supports the source. Other files may need browser-ready delivery first. That can delay playback.

## Bookmarks in books

In the book reader, open **Bookmarks and highlights** and choose **Bookmark this page**. A bookmark marks the page you are on. A small **Global** tag marks a bookmark shared with everyone who can see the book. Administrators also see **Everyone's** (other people's bookmarks in this book, with their names) and a **Promote** button on each personal bookmark; promoting makes it Global at once and folds in personal bookmarks from the same paragraph, so a place is never listed twice. Only an administrator deletes a Global bookmark. Bookmarks you made before this arrived are still yours.

**Find bookmarks** is in the book reader too, beside **Bookmark this page** in the same panel, and works like the one for films: choose **Your file**, pick a bookmark file someone made for the same book, then compare. Each incoming bookmark sits beside the nearest one you have, with a label and how far apart they are: **Same spot** (the same paragraph), **Close** (the same chapter) or **Far** (up to two chapters away); anything else is **New**. Tap a card and the reader jumps to that place, and that card is the one you keep. As elsewhere, **Same spot** keeps the incoming bookmark, **Close** and **Far** keep yours, **New** is added, and nothing changes until you press **Apply**. If you are an administrator your apply updates Global, with **Replace the whole Global set** if you want the file to take over; for everyone else it only changes their own bookmarks. A file made on another edition of the book (a different file, so different paragraphs) is shown as **Different edition**; choose **Align by chapter** and each bookmark lands at the start of the chapter with the same name. Administrators can also **Export Global bookmarks** for a book.

## Bookmarks in videos and audiobooks

In the video player, choose **Bookmarks**. The pane opens above the bar, tall enough to browse a long list. **Add bookmark here** (it shows the time) saves a personal bookmark in one click; type a note first if you like, and press Enter to save it with the note. There is no keyboard shortcut for bookmarks, so a stray key press never adds one.

Under the add row the pane lists the title's bookmarks in time order. When the title has both kinds, a filter appears: **All**, **Mine** or **Global**. Library remembers your choice on your profile. **Mine** are yours alone. **Global** bookmarks are shared with everyone who can see the title; they are read-only for everyone except an administrator, who can rename, edit (name, note, longer description) and delete them. Each row has **Play** and, for ones you may change, **Delete** (it asks "Delete?" in place).

An administrator on a computer or tablet also sees **Everyone's**. It lists every profile's personal bookmarks with the owner's name, and **Promote** turns one into a Global bookmark at once, with no confirmation (a short glow marks the row). Promoting a bookmark replaces personal bookmarks within three seconds of it, so a moment is never listed twice. Everyone's is not offered in the Vault, on a phone or on a TV.

**Find bookmarks** sits under the **Show bookmarks on the bar** switch (computers and tablets, not the Vault, not a TV). It opens a sub-view of the same tall pane, with a back arrow. Today it has one source, **Your file**: choose a bookmark file someone made for the same film, show or video. Library tells you whether it is for this title and this cut (**Matches this cut**, **Different cut**, or **Not this title**); for a different cut, **Align by chapter** moves each bookmark to the chapter of the same name.

**Compare with your bookmarks** then lists each incoming bookmark beside the nearest one you already have, as two cards: **Incoming** and **Yours**. A label shows how close they are, with the gap beside it: **Same spot** (up to 3 seconds), **Close** (up to 30 seconds), **Far** (up to 3 minutes) or **New** (nothing nearby). Tap a card and the player jumps to that spot, and that card becomes the one you keep (it gets a tick). Without any clicking the sensible choice is already made: **Same spot** keeps the incoming bookmark, **Close** and **Far** keep yours, and **New** is added (tap **Leave out** to skip one). Nothing changes until you press **Apply**. If you are an administrator, what you keep from the file becomes Global, an incoming bookmark replaces the nearest Global one, and **Replace the whole Global set with this one** swaps the lot; for everyone else the bookmarks become their own and never replace a Global one. The gaps are not hard-coded; see [`config/bookmarks.json`](../reference/configuration.md#configbookmarksjson).

Administrators also get **Export Global bookmarks** in the same view. It saves this title's Global bookmarks, and only those, as a file for another Library. There is no export of personal bookmarks and no bulk import; see [Bookmark files](operations-and-recovery.md#bookmark-files).

By default the progress bar stays clean. Switch on **Show bookmarks on the bar** in the pane (or in **Settings > Playback**) to see a small round bubble for each bookmark: the accent colour for yours, white for Global. Point at a bubble (or tap it on a touch screen) to see its name, time and note, with Play; if it has a longer description, choose **More**. Chapter ticks are off for video by default and on for audiobooks; both are in **Settings > Playback**.

The audiobook player keeps its panel with **Add** and **Saved**. Add captures the moment the panel opens, so it is not lost while you type. It shows the chapter name when there is one, lets you preview the captured spot, and takes a short note (up to 200 characters). Saved lists the title's bookmarks, newest first, with jump-to and delete. Global bookmarks show "· Global", and the same **Find bookmarks** and (for administrators) **Everyone's** with **Promote** appear below the list. The audiobook progress bar draws chapter ticks but no bookmark bubbles, so there is nothing to switch off there.

Adding a bookmark works on a phone or tablet and does not need Editing Mode.

## Resume and save separately

Continue shelves and resume actions follow progress. Completed items show their completion state. Albums, tracks, and View items do not use long-form completion bars.

**My List** saves an item for later. **Rate** means Like or Dislike. **Favorite** is a separate choice. A song's heart adds it to Favorites. These choices do not start playback or replace progress.
