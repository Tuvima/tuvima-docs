# Editing photos, videos, documents and galleries

Photos, videos, documents and galleries share one editor, and it looks and behaves like the editor you use for movies, shows and music. Open it with **Edit**: from the viewer, from a document card, or from the selection bar after you tick several items. It opens as a window over the page you were on, and the page behind it does not change until you save.

Editing is available on a computer with a mouse or trackpad. On a phone, tablet or TV you can look at everything, but the Edit button does not appear. Fine corrections need a precise pointer and a big screen, and keeping them on the computer means a stray tap can never rewrite a caption.

## What you can change

**One photo or video:** description, date taken, place, people, tags and the galleries it belongs to. **One document:** title, correspondent, type, date, archive number, notes, tags and your own custom fields. **A gallery:** its name and description, how photos join it (by hand or by rule), its cover and an optional soundtrack.

The window has the same parts every time, in the same frame as the movie and music editor:

- **Header:** a picture of the item (the photo, a still from the video, or a document's first page) and a line that names it, such as "Photo · May 12, 2026 · Lisbon".
- **Tabs:** Details and History. A gallery has Details, Artwork and History.
- **Quick Facts:** a read-only card beside the form with the file name, type, size, pages or dimensions, and the number of parts, so you always know what you are looking at. There is no separate Files tab, because these facts are always in view.
- **Footer:** a red **Delete** at the left, then **Discard** and **Save changes** at the right. The footer also says "File write-back off": edits are saved in Library and your original files stay unchanged.

Facts that came from the file itself start locked: the date taken, the place and the people found in the photo. Press **Unlock** to change one. A small arrow beside it then puts it back to what the file says. The description, tags and galleries are yours, so they are never locked. Locking stops a stray click from overwriting what the camera recorded, while keeping your own words one click away.

## Why nothing is saved until you press Save changes

Typing in a field only stages the change. You can change five things, think better of it, and press **Discard**: nothing happened. When you press **Save changes**, Library first shows **Review changes**: one line per change, with what it was and what it will be. You can take a line out, or go back and edit. Saving then happens as one step, all or nothing. If one item cannot be changed (for example a document you are not allowed to edit), Library says which one and why, and nothing is changed for the rest, so a set of photos is never left half-edited.

The one exception is **Delete**. It asks "Are you sure?" once and then happens at once, because a deleted item goes to the Recycle bin where you can bring it back. Deleting a gallery never deletes its photos: they stay in your library.

## Editing many items at once

Tick several photos and choose **Edit**. The header says how many you picked, the dates they span and their formats (for example "36 JPEG · 4 RAW + JPEG"). The window offers only what makes sense for all of them: tags, people, date taken and place for photos and videos; tags, correspondent, type and date for documents. A caption, a title, an archive number or notes belong to one item, so they are not offered for a group. Where the items differ, the field says "Mixed, leave unchanged" and stays that way unless you set it. On a group only the place starts locked: the date is a shift and people are added, so neither can overwrite a single photo's own value by accident.

For the date you can **set** one date for all, or **shift** every date by the same amount. Shifting is for the camera whose clock was wrong: move every photo forward by three hours and each keeps its own time of day. Review tells you when a shift would move photos onto another day of the Timeline.

## Your files are never rewritten

Everything you change here is kept in Library, not written into the photo or document file. That is a privacy and safety choice: your originals stay exactly as they were, and a mistake in Library can never damage the only copy of a family photo. It also means files in a Read-only folder can still be tagged, captioned and sorted. A Read-only folder only stops the things that would touch the file itself, such as moving it to the Recycle bin, and the editor says so.

## Galleries from inside the editor

The **Galleries** field on a photo lets you add it to a gallery, take it out, or create a new gallery on the spot. Like everything else, it waits for Save changes. Favorite, Hide, Archive and Add to gallery from a card or the viewer stay one click, because they lose nothing and flip straight back; a slim ribbon confirms what happened instead of a pop-up that disappears.

When you open a photo's editor from inside a gallery, the gallery is the photo's family, the way a show is an episode's family. The header has a button with the gallery's name that opens the gallery itself, and a list of the gallery's photos with small pictures, showing where you are ("12 of 240"). The first eight photos are in the list, and **All photos** opens the rest. Pick another photo to move through the gallery without closing the window. Changes you staged on one photo are kept while you look at another, and Library asks before leaving if something is still unsaved. A photo that is not in a gallery has no family controls.

A new gallery made from a selection shows "Gallery · 40 photos" in its header, and its photos are listed the same way. How many photos the list shows before **All photos** is set in Library's editor configuration, not fixed in the app.

A gallery's cover is chosen by clicking a picture to make it the **Preferred** one, or by choosing **Automatic** and letting Library pick.

## Quick ways in, and one order everywhere

You can reach the editor without ticking anything first. On a computer, each photo in View has a small "..." menu with **Edit** and **Trash**, and the viewer's Gallery list ends with **New gallery**, which starts a gallery with the photo you are looking at.

The actions on a photo, video or document are always in the same order wherever you see them: Favorite, Add to gallery, Download, Share, Archive, Edit, Trash. Library shows only the ones that apply, but never in a different order, so you learn them once.

Beside every field you have changed, a small arrow puts it back. Nothing is saved either way until you press Save changes.

A saved Documents view opens in the same editor from its "..." menu. You can rename it and describe it there. Its filters are changed on the Documents page, because they depend on what you are looking at, and saving the view again keeps them.
