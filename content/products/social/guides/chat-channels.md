A **chat channel** is a live chat room inside a community. It sits beside the community's forums. Forums are for conversations worth keeping; channels are for quick talk as it happens. New messages appear on your screen without reloading the page.

Not every community has chat. The community's owner decides, and chooses the channels.

## Who can see a channel

Chat channels are for **members only**.

- Visitors never see them, even in a public community. Someone who has not joined does not see the **Chat** tab or the name of any channel.
- Channels and their messages never show on the Tuvima front page, the Home feed, Explore or search engines.
- The owner can open a channel to only some members. You only see the channels you can read.

## Open a channel

1. Open a community you have joined.
2. Choose the **Chat** tab.
3. Choose a channel. Channels have a `#` mark, such as **# general**, and are listed under their category next to that category's forums.

On a computer the list of channels stays beside the open channel, and the channel uses the full height of the screen. The community's name at the top of the list takes you back to the community's home page. On a phone the list is one screen and the channel is the next.

Some communities open on chat: when you open the community as a member, you land on the **Chat** tab. The **Home** tab still takes you to the community's home page.

The top of a channel shows its name, what it is for, and **Writing as** with the persona you use in that community.

## The dot for new messages

A channel with messages you have not read is shown in bold with a **dot**. Opening the channel clears it.

If someone replied to you or mentioned you there, the dot becomes a **number**: how many of those messages are waiting. Muted channels never show a number.

The **Chat** tab shows a dot too when a channel you have not muted has something new.

## Send a message

Type in the box at the bottom and choose **Send**. On a computer, Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line.

- A message can be up to 4,000 characters, with the same basic styling as posts, such as bold, links and lists.
- Photos cannot be sent in chat yet.
- You write as the one persona you use in that community.
- The community's rules apply as they do in forums. A suspension stops you writing in chat too, and [newcomer limits](newcomers.md) count chat messages and forum posts together. If the community blocks certain words, a message containing them is not sent and you are told why.

In a very busy channel you may be told the conversation is busy and asked to try again in a moment. Your message was not sent; send it again.

In a brand-new channel you see "Speak, friend, and enter." Be the first to say something.

## Reply, react, edit and remove

Choose the **Message options** button (three dots) beside a message.

- **Reply** quotes the message above yours.
- **React** with one of the emoji at the top of the options. Choose it again to take it back.
- **Change the words** edits your own message. It is marked **edited**, and everyone in the channel can choose **See the edits** to see what it said before.
- **Remove message** takes away your own message. A line saying "This message was removed" stays in its place, so people can see that something was there but not what it said.

Moderators can remove anyone's message. Their own messages in a channel carry a **Moderator** tag.

## Mention someone

Type `@` and the first letters of a member's handle, then pick them from the list. They get a notice in their bell, as long as they can read that channel and have not muted it. Only people you pick from the list are mentioned.

When someone replies to your message, you get a notice in the same way. A notice says who mentioned you or replied, and in which channel, never what was written.

## Choose your alerts

Choose the **info** button at the top of the channel. On a wide screen the same choices show in a column beside the channel. Under **Your alerts**:

- **Normal**: you are told about mentions and replies to you.
- **Muted**: you are told nothing from this channel.

Channel alerts appear in the bell only. They never send an email or a phone alert. There is no setting to be alerted about every message. See [keep up with conversations](keep-up.md).

## Search a channel

Choose the **search** button (the magnifying glass) at the top of the channel, type at least 2 letters and choose **Search**. Results show newest first. Choose one to open the channel at that message, or **Show older results** to go further back.

Search looks inside one channel at a time. Removed messages are never found.

## Report a message

Open **Message options** on someone else's message and choose **Report this message**. Pick what is wrong and send it.

- The report goes to the community's moderators.
- Before you send it, you see what they will receive: the message and up to 5 messages before and after it.

See [reporting and moderation](reporting-and-moderation.md).

## Read-only channels and slow mode

- In a **read-only** channel, only some members can post, usually moderators. It is used for things like announcements. The channel list marks it **read only**, and in place of the message box you see "Only some members can post here". You can still read and react.
- With **slow mode** on, each member waits a set time between their own messages, for example one message every 30 seconds. A line under the message box tells you when you can write again.

## Turn chat into a forum conversation

Chat moves fast. When something is worth keeping, turn it into a forum conversation.

1. Open **Message options** on the message and choose **Turn into a conversation**.
2. Give it a **Title** and choose a **Forum** you can post in.
3. Under **Messages to keep**, tick the messages to include, up to 20. You see how the conversation will open.
4. Choose **Start conversation**.

What happens:

- A new conversation starts in that forum. Its first post quotes each message, and each person is credited for their own words.
- A line with a link to the new conversation is left in the channel.
- The original messages stay in the channel.
- The conversation is added to **Kept from this channel** in the channel's info, which lists the newest 10. You only see conversations in forums you can open.

You can turn **your own** messages into a conversation. Turning anyone else's needs a right the community's owner gives to roles; moderators have it to begin with. You need to be able to post in the channel, so in a read-only channel only the people who can post there, and moderators, see this choice. The usual limits on starting conversations apply.

## Older messages may be cleared

A community's owner can set a channel to clear old messages automatically after 30 days, 90 days or a year. Cleared messages are deleted for good. If a channel does this, it says so under **How this channel works** in the channel's info. Conversations made from chat messages stay in their forums.

The owner can also delete a whole channel, which deletes its messages.

## How private is a channel?

We want to be plain about this.

- Everyone in the community who can open a channel can read everything in it, including members who join later.
- The community's owner and moderators can read every channel they have access to, and can remove messages.
- Channel messages are **not** end-to-end encrypted, the kind of protection where even the company running a service cannot read them. Like everything else stored on Tuvima, they can be read by the Tuvima team.
- Anyone who can read a message can copy or screenshot it.

For something meant for one person or a small group, use [private messages](messages.md). Read more in [your privacy on Tuvima](privacy.md).
