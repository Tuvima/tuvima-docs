Most people who make things online earn in one of two ways. Some get a share of advertising on a large platform. Others charge for subscriptions on their own website. Both work, but both leave something out.

- **Advertising shares** depend on reach. The platform decides who sees your work, and you earn from attention rather than from the people who care most.
- **Your own subscriptions** keep the relationship with you, but only a small part of your audience will ever pay for one. The rest read, watch or listen and then leave.

Tuvima doesn't ask anyone to give up what already works. It adds a place where your most engaged people gather, talk to you and each other, and can support you in small ways as well as big ones. You keep your channel, your website and your subscribers. Tuvima is **in addition to** them.

## Story 1: a newsroom

*Harbor Daily is a regional news site. Its income comes from subscriptions on its own website. Readers who don't subscribe hit the paywall and leave, and the comment section is mostly arguments.*

**Before Tuvima.** A reader loves an investigation by Ana, one of the reporters. They don't want a full subscription, so there's no way for them to say thank you. Ana never hears from them.

**With Tuvima.** Harbor Daily opens a community on Tuvima and links each article to its discussion.

1. That same reader gives the article a gift for a couple of dollars, or sends Ana a $5 tip. Neither needs a subscription.
2. Harbor Daily's rule on Trove Business says reporters keep 70% of what readers send them. Ana sees her share on her eligibility page, and the newsroom pays it with her salary.
3. The discussion happens in Harbor Daily's own community, with its own rules and moderators. Readers have a reason to come back, not just a comment box under a story.
4. The newsroom sells a paid tier in the community, such as "Insider" with a members-only chat with the editors, and keeps 80% of it.
5. Trove members who value local news give Harbor Daily their monthly Ember. That costs the reader nothing extra.

**What changed.** Subscriptions still come in. On top of them, the readers who would never subscribe now have a small, easy way to pay. Reporters see their work rewarded directly, and the newsroom gets a community it controls. See [Organisations and publishers](organisations-and-publishers.md).

### When readers share your articles

Today, when someone shares a news article on a big discussion site, the person who shared it collects the upvotes and the attention. The newsroom that paid for the reporting gets nothing, except perhaps the hope that one reader in thousands subscribes.

On Tuvima it works differently. Say a member shares Ana's investigation in a car enthusiasts' community. People there discuss it and give gifts to the article.

- **Gifts on the article go to Harbor Daily,** wherever it's shared and however many times it's reshared. The person who shared it doesn't become the owner of it just by posting the link.
- **Gifts on someone's comment go to that commenter,** as you'd expect. Good discussion is rewarded too.
- **Ana gets her share** under the newsroom's staff rule, so the reporter benefits even when her work travels far from home.

To start receiving these gifts, Harbor Daily claims its website by showing it controls it. Gifts given before the claim don't move to the publisher afterwards, so it pays to claim early.

### Bringing your subscribers with you

On Enterprise, Harbor Daily can sell its real subscription through Tuvima. A reader who subscribes in Harbor Daily's Tuvima community gets both the community perks, such as the Insider chat, and full access on harbordaily.com. With the reader's permission, Tuvima ID simply tells Harbor Daily's site that this reader has an active subscription, and nothing else about them.

People who subscribe on harbordaily.com itself don't get Tuvima perks automatically. Harbor Daily can include them by sharing part of that subscription with Tuvima, the same way it would for a subscription sold on Tuvima. Either way, Tuvima keeps track of who qualifies, so perks keep working even if the newsroom's own site is briefly down.

## Story 2: a video creator

*Leo makes videos about restoring old bikes. His income is a share of the advertising on the big video site he posts to, plus the odd sponsor. He has a large audience but hardly knows any of them.*

**Before Tuvima.** His income goes up and down with whatever the video site decides to show people. His most loyal viewers are mixed in with millions of passers-by, and the comments are hard to keep up with.

**With Tuvima.** Leo keeps posting videos where he always has. He opens a Tuvima community for the people who want more.

1. **Closer to fans.** His community has chat channels, a "Show your build" forum and a members' tier with workshop live chats. The people there chose to be there.
2. **Earning from the people who care.** Fans give gifts on his posts and send tips. Leo keeps 65% of every gift and 85% of every tip, and the community's own 5% share goes to his community too.
3. **Growing his share.** As more people support him, he moves up the creator levels. At 1,000 supporters he's a Torchbearer: he keeps 70% of gifts and gets his own signature gift. At 10,000 he keeps 72%.
4. **Monthly support from members.** Each month, Trove members choose who to support with their Ember, and many choose him. It costs the fan nothing extra.
5. **Better tools.** New members get a welcome path (the Trailhead), achievements to collect and levels to climb, so newcomers find their feet without Leo answering the same questions again and again.

**What changed.** The advertising money still arrives. On top of it, Leo now has a fan community he runs himself, income that doesn't depend on reach, and supporters he actually knows. See [Earning on Tuvima](earning.md).

## Story 3: a hobby community

*The Map Makers is a small group of fantasy cartographers. It has no budget, just a few volunteers who keep it running.*

**With Tuvima.** The group earns 5% of every gift given in it, rising to 6% and then 8% as it grows. Trove members support it with Ember. The owners use the money to run competitions with prizes. Nobody is charged to join, and volunteers are never counted as paid seats. See [Earning for your community](community-earnings.md).

## What makes Tuvima different

| | Earning only from advertising | Only your own website | Adding Tuvima |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who pays you | Advertisers, based on reach | Subscribers only | Subscribers, plus anyone who gives a gift, tip or Ember |
| When others share your work | They get the credit | Nothing comes back | Gifts on your work still come to you |
| How easy it is for a fan to give something small | Hard | Hard | Easy: small gifts and tips, no subscription needed |
| Who your audience is | Mostly passers-by | Your subscribers | The people who chose to join your community |
| Who sets the rules | The platform | You | You, in your own community |
| Can paying get you more reach? | Often | Not applicable | Only in clearly labelled Promoted spaces. Organic feeds give a small, capped lift for many supporters, never for more money |

## Tools that come with it

- **Your own community**, with forums, chat channels, roles and your own look. See [Run a community](../owners/run-your-community.md).
- **A welcome path for newcomers**, the Trailhead, so new members settle in quickly. See [The Trailhead](../owners/trailhead.md).
- **Achievements and levels** that reward taking part. See [Achievements](../owners/achievements.md).
- **Paid member tiers** for perks such as members-only places. See [Member tiers](../owners/member-tiers.md).
- **Add-ons**, from Polls to tools built by other developers, that you switch on as you need them. See [Add-ons](../owners/add-ons.md).
- **Planned:** putting your community's conversations on your own website, and moving your community to your own web address.

## What Tuvima never does

- It never sells a higher place in normal feeds. Gifts, tips and plans give you money and tools. Paid promotion stays in labelled Promoted spaces, and a community with many supporters gets only a small, capped lift ([Short names and Watchfire](short-names-and-watchfire.md#how-support-affects-what-people-see)).
- It never takes over your existing channels. You keep posting wherever you already do.
- It never shows your supporters' private details. Gifts are anonymous unless the giver chooses otherwise.
